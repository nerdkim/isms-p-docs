## Certification criterion

A privacy policy must be established in a way that is easy for data subjects to understand, including all necessary matters such as the purpose of processing personal data. It must be disclosed by appropriate means so that data subjects can easily check it at any time, and it must be kept continuously up to date.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is the privacy policy written specifically and clearly in easy-to-understand terms, including all the matters required by law?
2. Is the privacy policy disclosed on an Internet website or similar channel and kept continuously up to date so that data subjects can easily check it?
3. When the privacy policy is changed, are the reasons and the details of the change announced without delay, and are measures taken so that data subjects can easily identify the changed matters at any time?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special provisions for Information Security Management System certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the official text as relaxed/adjusted, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.5.1 Disclosure of the Privacy Policy] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

**The privacy policy must be written specifically and clearly in easy-to-understand terms, including all the matters required by law.**

- Mandatory matters to be included in the privacy policy (refer to Article 30 of the Personal Information Protection Act and Article 31 of the Enforcement Decree of the same Act)
  1. The purpose of processing personal data
  2. The processing and retention period of personal data
  3. Matters concerning the provision of personal data to third parties (specified only where applicable)
  4. Procedures and methods for destroying personal data (where personal data must be retained pursuant to the proviso of Article 21(1) of the Act, the retention grounds and the personal data items retained are included)
  5. The possibility of disclosure of sensitive information under Article 23(3) of the Act and the method of choosing non-disclosure (specified only where applicable)
  6. Matters concerning the outsourcing of personal data processing (specified only where applicable)
  7. Matters concerning the processing of pseudonymized information under Articles 28-2 and 28-3 of the Act (specified only where applicable)
  8. Matters concerning the rights/obligations of the data subject and the legal representative, and how to exercise them
  9. The name of the personal data protection officer under Article 31, or the name of the department that handles personal data protection duties and related grievances, and its contact information such as telephone number
  10. Matters concerning the installation/operation of, and refusal of, devices that automatically collect personal data, such as Internet access log files (specified only where applicable)
  11. The items of personal data processed
  12. Matters concerning measures to ensure the security of personal data under Article 30 of the Enforcement Decree
- Other matters to be included in the privacy policy (Article 19 of the Standard Personal Data Protection Guidelines)
  1. Where cross-border transfer is necessary in order to outsource the processing of, or store, personal data under Article 28-8(1)3 of the Act, the matters set out in each subparagraph of Article 28-8(2) of the Act (specified only where applicable)
  2. Matters concerning changes to the privacy policy
  3. Where a domestic agent is designated under Article 31-2(1) of the Act, the name, address, telephone number, and email address of the domestic agent (specified only where applicable)
  4. Matters concerning the rights/obligations of the data subject and the legal representative and how to exercise them, such as the right to request access to, correction/deletion of, and suspension of processing of personal data
  5. The department that receives and handles requests for access to personal data
  6. Remedies for infringement of the data subject's rights and interests
- For personal data that may be processed without the data subject's consent, its items and the legal grounds for processing must be disclosed in the privacy policy separately from personal data processed with the data subject's consent.
- The content of the privacy policy, such as the purpose of processing personal data, the items of personal data processed, and matters concerning third-party provision, must match the actual status of personal data processing, and must be written specifically and clearly in easy-to-understand terms, reflecting the characteristics of the service and the data subjects.
  1. Establish the matters that the law requires to be included in the privacy policy, such as the grounds for processing personal data and the guarantee of data subject rights, specifically and appropriately
  2. Establish the privacy policy in clear and easy-to-understand language so that data subjects can readily understand it
- Where an exception under the Personal Information Protection Act applies, a privacy policy may not need to be established.

  Note: Personal data files (public institutions) exempted from establishing a privacy policy
  1. Personal data files recording matters concerning national security, diplomatic secrets, or other significant national interests
  2. Personal data files recording matters concerning criminal investigation, the institution and maintenance of prosecution, the execution of sentences and custody, correctional dispositions, protective dispositions, security surveillance dispositions, and immigration control
  3. Personal data files recording matters concerning investigations of offenses under the "Punishment of Tax Evaders Act" and investigations of offenses under the "Customs Act"
  4. Personal data files that are recognized as having a low need for continuous management, such as files operated on a one-off basis, as prescribed by Presidential Decree
     - Personal data files operated to carry out simple tasks such as paying meeting attendance allowances, sending materials/goods, and settling money, which have a low need for continuous management
     - Personal data files processed temporarily in cases urgently necessary for public safety and welfare, such as public health
     - Other personal data files collected only for one-off task processing that are not stored or recorded
  5. Personal data files classified as secret under other statutes

**The privacy policy must be disclosed on an Internet website or similar channel and kept continuously up to date, ensuring accessibility so that data subjects can easily check it.**

- Use the standardized name "privacy policy".
- When disclosing it on the first screen of the Internet website, display it so that data subjects can easily identify it by distinguishing it from other notices using font size, color, and the like.
- Where no Internet website is operated, the privacy policy may be disclosed through other methods prescribed by law.

  Note: Methods of disclosing the privacy policy where it cannot be posted on an Internet website (Article 31(3) of the Enforcement Decree)
  - Posting it in a conspicuous place at the personal data controller's place of business, etc.
  - Publishing it in the official gazette (only where the personal data controller is a public institution), or in a general daily newspaper, general weekly newspaper, or Internet newspaper under Article 2, subparagraph 1, items (a)/(c) and subparagraph 2 of the "Act on the Promotion of Newspapers, Etc." whose main circulation area is the region at or above the city/province level where the personal data controller's place of business, etc. is located
  - Continuously including it in a publication/newsletter/promotional material or bill, etc. issued at least twice a year under the same title and distributed to data subjects
  - Including it in a contract, etc. prepared by the personal data controller and the data subject to provide goods or services, and issuing it to the data subject
**When the privacy policy is changed, the reasons and the details of the change must be announced without delay, and measures must be taken so that data subjects can easily identify the changed matters at any time.**

- Disclose the policy by comparing the before/after versions so that data subjects can easily check the changed matters at any time.

  Note: Methods of announcing the reasons for and content of a change to the privacy policy (examples)
  - Announcing it through the notice section on the first screen of the Internet website or through a separate window
  - Announcing it to data subjects in writing/by fax/by email or by a similar method
  - Writing and posting it or making it available in a conspicuous place inside a store/office

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 30 (Establishment and Disclosure of the Privacy Policy), Article 30-2 (Evaluation of the Privacy Policy and Recommendation for Improvement)

## Evidence (examples)

- Privacy policy
- Records of notices concerning amendments to the privacy policy (bulletin board, etc.)

## Nonconformity examples

- Where the personal data collection and third-party provision details disclosed in the privacy policy differ from the details actually collected and provided
- Where changes have occurred in the content disclosed in the privacy policy, such as a change of the personal data protection officer or a change of an outsourcee, but the policy has not been updated to reflect them
- Where the privacy policy is disclosed, but its name is "Personal Data Protection Policy" rather than "Privacy Policy," and it is not displayed using font size, color, and the like so that data subjects can easily find it
- Where the privacy policy has been amended several times, but the content of previously prepared privacy policies is not disclosed in a way that allows it to be checked
- Where personal data is retained for a certain period without being destroyed under other statutes such as the Electronic Commerce Act and the Commercial Act, but the retention grounds and the personal data items retained accordingly are not disclosed in the privacy policy
