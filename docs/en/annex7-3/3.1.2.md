## Certification criterion

When collecting personal information, only the minimum personal information necessary for the processing purpose shall be collected, and the provision of goods or services to the data subject shall not be refused on the grounds that the data subject has not consented to matters to which they may selectively consent.

## Key checkpoints

1. When collecting personal information, is only the minimum information within the scope necessary for the purpose being collected?
2. When collecting personal information with the consent of the data subject, is the fact that they may decline to consent to the collection of personal information beyond the minimum necessary information being specifically communicated?
3. Is it ensured that the provision of services or goods is not refused on the grounds that the data subject does not consent to the collection of personal information beyond the minimum information necessary for the collection purpose?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the officially relaxed/adjusted text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.1.2 Limitation on Collection of Personal Information] (under the relaxed criteria, it applies within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

When collecting personal information, only the minimum information within the scope necessary for the purpose, such as a legal basis, compliance with legal obligations, or the conclusion/performance of a contract, shall be collected.

- Even when personal information is collected with the consent of the data subject, or without the data subject's consent on the basis of a legal basis, compliance with legal obligations, or the conclusion/performance of a contract, only the minimum personal information necessary for the purpose shall be collected.
- Since the burden of proof regarding the minimum personal information rests with the personal information controller, it must be possible to prove that the information collected as mandatory is the minimum personal information necessary for providing the service, etc. (here, the minimum personal information means information that is essential for the fundamental function of the relevant service).

Note: Minimum information (examples)
- The name, address, telephone number, etc. collected by a shopping company to deliver goods to a customer can be regarded as the necessary minimum personal information, but requiring personal information unrelated to delivery, such as occupation or date of birth, goes beyond the scope of the minimum information.
- Requiring, from customers who have entered a prize event, information beyond that necessary to notify them of the prize drawing, such as the applicant's gender, number of children, whether they live with others, or other information concerning the secrecy of private life, or unique identifying information, goes beyond the scope of the minimum information.
- Information about a job applicant's career, major, certifications, etc. can be regarded as the minimum information for judging their job competency, but information about family relationships, marital status, place of origin (permanent domicile), etc. goes beyond the minimum information.

When collecting personal information with the consent of the data subject, the fact that they may decline to consent to the collection of personal information beyond the necessary minimum information shall be specifically communicated.

- Notify by distinguishing which information is the necessary minimum information and which is not, so that the data subject can easily identify it.
- Notify that, for information that is not the necessary minimum information, consent may be freely declined without any hindrance to the use of goods or services.

It shall be ensured that the provision of services or goods is not refused on the grounds that the data subject does not provide personal information beyond the minimum information necessary for the collection purpose.

- Notify by clearly indicating the fact that the service can be used even if the data subject declines to consent to optional items.
- Implement the system so that essential services such as membership registration can be used even if, during the membership registration process, the data subject does not consent to or does not enter optional information.
  - Note: For details, refer to the "Easy-to-Understand Guide on Consent to Personal Information Processing (Personal Information Protection Commission)".

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 16 (Restrictions on Collection of Personal Information)
- Personal Information Protection Act Article 22 (Methods of Obtaining Consent)

## Evidence (examples)

- Online personal information collection forms (website membership registration screens, event participation screens, etc.)
- Offline personal information collection forms (membership application forms, etc.)
- Personal information processing policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Collecting personal information without the data subject's consent on the basis of the conclusion and performance of a contract, while excessively collecting even personal information items that are not strictly necessary for the conclusion and performance of the contract.
- Obtaining consent to optional items from the data subject without specifically informing them that they may decline to consent to the collection of the relevant personal information.
- Although the membership registration form was set up to distinguish mandatory and optional information and to obtain separate consent, failing to specifically inform the data subject that membership registration is possible even without consenting to the optional information (such as cases where the mandatory/optional status is not indicated for each personal information item in the personal information entry form).
- Cases where, on the website membership registration screen, if the data subject does not consent to optional items or does not enter optional information, they cannot proceed to the next step or membership registration is blocked.
- Collecting excessive personal information, such as family details not directly related to the intended job, when entering into an employment contract.
