## Certification criterion

Personal information must be collected/used lawfully and legitimately, and where it is collected on the basis of the data subject's consent, the data subject's consent must be obtained by lawful means. In addition, where personal information of a child under the age of 14 is collected, the consent of the child's legal representative must be obtained and it must be verified whether the legal representative has consented.

## Key checkpoints

1. When collecting personal information, is it collected in accordance with lawful grounds such as the data subject's consent, compliance with legal obligations, and conclusion/performance of a contract?
2. When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, are the method and timing of consent appropriate?
3. When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, are the relevant matters clearly notified, and are the important matters prescribed by law clearly indicated so that they are easy to recognize?
4. When obtaining consent for the collection/use/provision, etc. of the personal information of a child under the age of 14, are the necessary matters notified to and consent obtained from the legal representative?
5. Is only the minimum personal information necessary to obtain the legal representative's consent collected, and are procedures and methods in place to verify whether the legal representative meets the eligibility requirements?
6. When notifying a child under the age of 14 of matters related to personal information processing, etc., are they expressed in an easy-to-understand format and in clear and easy-to-understand language?
7. Are records of consent obtained from the data subject and the legal representative retained?
8. For personal information that may be processed without the data subject's consent, are the items and the legal grounds for processing disclosed in the privacy policy or notified to the data subject, distinguished from personal information processed with the data subject's consent?
9. For additional use of personal information without the data subject's consent, have judgment criteria for considerations such as relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether it infringes on interests, and safety measures been established/implemented, and where additional use continuously occurs, are the judgment criteria for these considerations disclosed in the privacy policy and inspected?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special case for information security management system certification. The above certification criterion/key checkpoints are the relaxed/adjusted official text, and the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [3.1.1 Collection/Use of Personal Information], which is the same control area (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the above certification criterion/checkpoints).

When collecting personal information, the lawful requirements under relevant laws, such as the data subject's consent, compliance with legal obligations, and conclusion/performance of a contract, must be clearly identified, and personal information must be lawfully collected accordingly.

- Clearly identify the lawful requirements for collecting personal information by each collection channel, and record/manage the relevant grounds so that they can be proven
  - For example, where personal information is collected without the data subject's consent because there is a special provision in a statute or in order to comply with a legal obligation, document the relevant grounds such as the provision of the relevant statute or law
- A personal information controller may collect personal information in any of the following cases, and may use it within the scope of the collection purpose

  **Cases in which the collection of personal information is permitted (Personal Information Protection Act, Article 15(1))**
  1. Where the data subject's consent is obtained
  2. Where there is a special provision in a statute or it is unavoidable in order to comply with a legal obligation
  3. Where it is unavoidable for a public institution to perform its duties as prescribed by statutes, etc.
  4. Where it is necessary to perform a contract concluded with the data subject or to take measures requested by the data subject in the course of concluding a contract
  5. Where it is deemed clearly necessary for the urgent life, body, or property interests of the data subject or a third party
  6. Where it is necessary to achieve the legitimate interests of the personal information controller and such interests clearly override the rights of the data subject. In this case, it is limited to cases where the collection is substantially related to the legitimate interests of the personal information controller and does not exceed a reasonable scope.
  7. Where it is urgently necessary for public safety and welfare, such as public health

When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, consent must be obtained by an appropriate method reflecting the characteristics of the personal information collection medium, and the information must be collected at the point in time when it is needed.

- Consent to the collection of personal information must be obtained from the data subject by an appropriate method, taking into account the following matters according to the characteristics of the collection medium

  **Methods of obtaining consent to the processing of personal information (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act, Article 17(2))**
  1. A method of directly issuing to the data subject a written document stating the content of consent, or delivering it by mail, fax, etc., and receiving a consent form signed or sealed by the data subject
  2. A method of informing the data subject of the content of consent by telephone and confirming the expression of intent to consent
  3. A method of informing the data subject of the content of consent by telephone, having the data subject confirm the matters of consent through an internet address, etc., and then confirming the expression of intent to consent to those matters again by telephone
  4. A method of posting the content of consent on an internet homepage, etc. and having the data subject indicate whether they consent
  5. A method of sending an e-mail stating the content of consent and receiving from the data subject an e-mail stating the expression of intent to consent
  6. In addition, a method of informing the content of consent and confirming the expression of intent to consent by a method equivalent to those under subparagraphs 1 through 5

- Personal information must not be collected comprehensively in advance at the membership registration stage; it must be collected at the point in time when the information is needed
  - Consent to collection/use must be obtained only for the personal information necessary to start the service, and for services provided thereafter, consent must be obtained at the point in time when that service is provided
  - When registering as a member of a website, personal information needed only for the use of a specific service within the website should be collected at the point in time when that service is used
  - However, in the case of a recurring service, where consent is obtained at the point in time of the first use of the service by classifying it as an optional consent item, collection/use is permitted

When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, the statutory matters to be notified must be clearly notified and consent obtained, and the important matters prescribed by law must be clearly indicated so that the data subject can easily recognize them.

- When obtaining the data subject's consent to the collection/use of personal information, the four statutory matters to be notified must be specifically and clearly informed and consent obtained

  **Matters to be notified when obtaining consent to the collection/use of personal information (Personal Information Protection Act, Article 15(2))**
  1. The purpose of collection/use of the personal information
  2. The items of personal information to be collected
  3. The retention and use period of the personal information
  4. The fact that the data subject has the right to refuse consent, and, where there is any disadvantage resulting from the refusal of consent, the content of such disadvantage

- For the data subject's consent to be lawful, it must satisfy all of the lawful requirements, such as the data subject deciding whether to consent based on their free will and the specificity and clarity of the content of consent

  **Conditions to be satisfied when obtaining the data subject's consent (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act, Article 17(1))**
  1. The data subject shall be able to decide whether to consent based on their free will
  2. The content for which consent is sought shall be specific and clear
  3. Wording that can be easily read and understood shall be used for such content
  4. A method by which the data subject can clearly indicate whether they consent shall be provided to the data subject

  * However, this provision takes effect from September 15, 2024

- Pursuant to Article 22(2) of the Personal Information Protection Act (Method of obtaining consent), when consent to the processing of personal information is obtained in writing (including electronic documents under Article 2(1) of the Framework Act on Electronic Documents and Transactions), the important matters must be clearly indicated so as to be easy to recognize, as follows

  **Important matters that must be clearly indicated (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act, Article 17(3))**
  - Among the purposes of collection/use of personal information, the fact that the relevant personal information may be used to contact the data subject for the promotion or sale solicitation of goods or services, etc.
  - Among the items of personal information to be processed: sensitive information, passport number, driver's license number, alien registration number
  - The retention and use period of the personal information (in the case of provision, the retention and use period of the recipient)
  - The recipient of the personal information and the recipient's purpose of using the personal information

  **Method of indicating important matters (Notice on Methods of Processing Personal Information, Article 4)**
  - Ensure that the content is clearly indicated through the size, color, or thickness of the letters, or underlining, etc.
  - Where there are many matters of consent and the important content is difficult to clearly distinguish, indicate the important content separately from the other content so that it can be easily identified

  * Taking into account the characteristics of the medium requiring written consent, such as paper printouts or computer display screens, and the data subject's usage environment, etc., indicate it so that the data subject can easily recognize it

* For details, refer to the "Guide on Consent to Personal Information Processing (Personal Information Protection Commission)"

Where consent for the collection/use/provision, etc. of personal information of a child under the age of 14 is obtained, the necessary matters must be notified to the legal representative and consent obtained.

- Where there is no need to process the personal information of a child under the age of 14, take measures not to collect the personal information of a child under the age of 14 through an appropriate age verification procedure
- Where there is a need to process the personal information of a child under the age of 14, take measures to obtain the legal representative's consent by establishing a separate collection consent form and a legal representative verification procedure

  **Methods of verifying whether the legal representative has consented (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act, Article 17-2(1))**
  1. A method of having the legal representative indicate whether they consent on an internet site posting the content of consent, and the personal information controller notifying the legal representative, by mobile phone text message, that it has confirmed the indication of consent
  2. A method of having the legal representative indicate whether they consent on an internet site posting the content of consent, and receiving card information such as the legal representative's credit card/debit card
  3. A method of having the legal representative indicate whether they consent on an internet site posting the content of consent, and verifying the identity through the legal representative's mobile phone identity authentication, etc.
  4. A method of directly issuing to the legal representative a written document stating the content of consent, or delivering it by mail or fax, and having the legal representative sign and seal the content of consent and submit it
  5. A method of sending an e-mail stating the content of consent and receiving from the legal representative an e-mail stating the expression of intent to consent
  6. A method of informing the legal representative of the content of consent by telephone and obtaining consent, or guiding a method by which the content of consent can be confirmed, such as an internet address, and obtaining consent through a further telephone call
  7. In addition, a method equivalent to those under subparagraphs 1 through 6 of informing the legal representative of the content of consent and confirming the expression of intent to consent

Only the minimum information necessary to obtain the legal representative's consent (information on name/contact details) must be collected, and procedures and methods to verify whether the legal representative meets the eligibility requirements must be established.

- The minimum information necessary to obtain the legal representative's consent (information on the legal representative's name/contact details) may be collected directly from the child without the legal representative's consent
  - However, when collecting the legal representative's name/contact details, the child must be informed of one's own identity and contact details, and the reason for collecting the legal representative's name and contact details (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines, Article 13(1))
  - The legal representative's personal information collected from the child must be used only for the purpose of obtaining consent
- To obtain the legal representative's consent, it must be verified whether the information provided by the child is that of the true legal representative and whether the legal representative is genuine
  - Verify whether the legal representative is a minor
  - Verify the age difference from the child, etc.
  - * (Reference) Legal representative of a minor
    - Primarily, the person with parental authority, such as the child's parent, is the legal representative (Civil Act, Article 911)
    - Where a minor has no parents or the parents cannot exercise parental authority, secondarily a guardian becomes the legal representative, and the guardian follows the order of designated guardian (Civil Act, Article 931) and appointed guardian (Civil Act, Article 932)
- Where the legal representative refuses consent or the legal representative's intent to consent is not confirmed, it must be destroyed within 5 days from the date of collection (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines, Article 13(2))

When notifying a child under the age of 14 of matters related to personal information processing, etc., they must be expressed in an easy-to-understand format and in clear and easy-to-understand language.

- Convey information transparently in a child-friendly manner, such as language, pictures, and videos that are easy for children to understand
- Take into account children's capabilities and usage behaviors by age group, etc.

  * For details, refer to the "Guidelines on the Protection of Personal Information of Children/Youth (Personal Information Protection Commission)"

Records of consent obtained from the data subject and the legal representative must be kept and preserved so that the lawful grounds for consent to the collection/use of personal information can be proven.

- Matters to be recorded: date and time of consent, items consented to, the consenting party (in the case of consent by the legal representative, the legal representative's information), method of consent, etc.
- Retention period: until the relevant personal information is destroyed due to membership withdrawal, etc.

For personal information that may be processed without the data subject's consent, the items and the legal grounds for processing must be disclosed in the privacy policy or notified to the data subject by a method such as in writing, distinguished from personal information processed with the data subject's consent.

- Cases in which personal information may be collected/used without the data subject's consent: cases falling under subparagraphs 2 through 7 of Article 15(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act
- Matters to be notified to the data subject: the items of personal information that may be processed without consent and the legal grounds for processing
- Method of notifying the data subject: disclose in the privacy policy, distinguished from personal information processed with consent, or notify the data subject by a method such as in writing (in writing, e-mail, fax, telephone, text message, or an equivalent method)

For additional use of personal information without the data subject's consent, judgment criteria for considerations such as relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether it infringes on interests, and safety measures must be established/implemented, and where additional use continuously occurs, these must be disclosed in the privacy policy and compliance with the criteria must be inspected.

  **Considerations for the additional use of personal information (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act, Article 14-2(1))**
  1. Whether there is relevance to the original collection purpose
  2. Whether there is foreseeability of the additional use or provision of the personal information in light of the circumstances of collection or the practices of processing
  3. Whether it unfairly infringes on the data subject's interests
  4. Whether measures necessary to ensure safety, such as pseudonymization or encryption, have been taken

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act, Article 15 (Collection/use of personal information), Article 22 (Method of obtaining consent), Article 22-2 (Protection of children's personal information)
- Notice on Methods of Processing Personal Information

## Evidence (examples)

- Online personal information collection forms (homepage membership registration screen, mobile app membership registration screen, event participation, etc.)
- Offline personal information collection forms (membership application forms, etc.)
- Records of consent to the collection of personal information (member database, etc.)
- Records of legal representative consent
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Where a personal information controller subject to the Personal Information Protection Act omits the "right to refuse consent and the content of disadvantages resulting from the refusal of consent" from the matters to be notified when obtaining consent to the collection of personal information
- Where, when obtaining consent to the collection of personal information, the items of personal information to be collected are not specifically stated but are guided comprehensively, such as "~, etc."
- Where, at the time of membership registration on a shopping mall homepage, payment/delivery information needed later for purchasing goods is collected in advance as mandatory items in addition to the personal information necessary for membership registration
- Where the personal information of non-members (name, e-mail, mobile phone number) is collected through Q&A or a bulletin board without going through a procedure for consent to the collection of personal information
- Where personal information of a child under the age of 14 is collected without obtaining the legal representative's consent
- Where, although the service is not provided to children under the age of 14, an age check is not performed using the date of birth entered at the membership registration stage, so there are members under the age of 14 who joined without the legal representative's consent
- Where the procedure for verifying whether the legal representative is genuine is inadequate, so that consent by the legal representative is possible even for someone who is difficult to regard as the legal representative of a child, such as a minor
- Where, after collecting the legal representative's personal information (name, mobile phone number) from a child under the age of 14 for the purpose of obtaining the legal representative's consent, the legal representative's consent has not been confirmed for a long time, yet it is not destroyed and continues to be retained
- Where personal information of a child under the age of 14 was collected on the basis of the legal representative's consent, but the relevant records were not preserved, so that matters related to the legal representative's consent (legal representative's name, date and time of consent, etc.) cannot be confirmed
