## Certification criterion

According to the organization's business characteristics, a classification standard for information assets shall be established to identify and classify all information assets within the scope of the management system, excluding physical infrastructure, and after assessing their importance, the list shall be kept up to date.

## Key checkpoints

1. Has a classification standard for information assets been established, and are all assets within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system identified and managed as a list?
2. For the identified information assets, is importance determined and a security grade assigned, taking into account legal requirements and impact on business?
3. Is the status of information assets surveyed periodically to keep the information asset list up to date?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [1.2.1 Information Asset Identification] (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

**A classification standard for information assets shall be established, and all assets within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system shall be identified and managed as a list.**

- Establish a classification standard for information assets suited to the organization's characteristics, and identify all information assets without omission according to the classification standard

  * Information asset classification (example)
  - Classification by asset type : servers, data (DBMS), information systems (application programs), software, network equipment, security systems, PCs, information, facilities, buildings, etc.
  - Items by asset type (example)
    - Server : host name, asset serial number, model name, purpose, IP address, managing department name, operational manager, responsible manager, security grade, etc.
    - Data : database name, table name, (personal) information item names (e.g., name, gender, date of birth, mobile phone number, email, etc.), managing department name, operational manager, responsible manager, storage system (host name), storage location (IP address), security grade, etc.
    - Information system : hardware and software required for the collection, processing, storage, retrieval, and transmission/reception of information, such as servers, terminals like PCs, auxiliary storage media, network equipment, and application programs
    - Security system : systems built to prevent the damage, alteration, or leakage of information, including intrusion prevention systems (firewalls), intrusion detection systems, intrusion prevention systems, personal information leakage prevention systems, etc.
    - Information : includes both documentary information and electronic information (important information, personal information, etc.)

- Compile the list by verifying asset information such as asset name, purpose, location, responsible person and manager, and managing department

- Manage information assets in various forms, such as using an asset management system or documents (Excel), for efficient management

- When using cloud services, prepare a classification standard that reflects the characteristics of cloud services (for example, virtual servers, object storage, etc.) and identify/manage cloud assets accordingly

**For the identified information assets, importance shall be determined and a security grade assigned, taking into account legal requirements and impact on business.**

- Determine a security grade evaluation standard suited to the characteristics of each asset, such as legal requirements or impact on business

  * Security grade assessment criteria (example)
  - Importance evaluation based on confidentiality, integrity, availability, legal compliance, etc.
  - Also consider service impact, loss of profit, loss of customers, damage to external image, etc.

- Assess the security grade of each information asset according to the security grade evaluation standard and manage it as a list

**The status of information assets shall be surveyed periodically to keep the information asset list up to date.**

- Establish procedures so that the status of newly introduced, changed, or disposed assets can be verified

- Conduct periodic surveys of the status of information assets and keep the information asset list up to date

## Related laws

_No individual legal provision directly corresponding to this item is specified._

## Evidence (examples)

- Information asset and personal information asset classification standard
- Information asset and personal information asset list (asset management system screen)
- Information asset and personal information security grades
- Asset audit records
- Risk analysis report (asset identification records)

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, in the asset list within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system, internal information leakage control systems used to control the PCs of important information handlers and personal information handlers, such as output security, document encryption, and USB media control, are omitted
- A case where there is personal information provided by a third party within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system, but asset identification for that personal information has not been performed
- A case where the information asset and personal information security grade classification standard specified in internal guidelines does not match the classification standard in the asset management ledger
- A case where on-premises assets have been identified, but the identification of IT services outsourced externally (web hosting, server hosting, cloud, etc.) is omitted (provided they are within the certification scope)
- A case where the rationality and reliability of information asset importance evaluation is insufficient, such as assessing the confidentiality grade of a backup server that stores personal information such as unique identification information as (Low)
