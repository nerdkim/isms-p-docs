## Certification criterion

Where personal data is not destroyed but retained pursuant to applicable laws even after the retention period has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, it must be limited to the minimum items necessary for that purpose and must be stored/managed separately from other personal data.

## Key checkpoints

1. Where personal data is retained pursuant to applicable laws without destruction even after the retention period has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, is it managed so that only the minimum information is retained, limited to the minimum period prescribed by the applicable laws?
2. Where personal data is retained pursuant to applicable laws without destruction even after the retention period has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, is that personal data or personal data file stored/managed separately from other personal data?
3. Is the separately stored personal data managed so that it can be processed only within the scope of the purpose prescribed by law?
4. Is access authority to the separately stored personal data restricted to the minimum number of personnel?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.4.2 Measures When Retaining Personal Data After the Processing Purpose Is Achieved] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

Where personal data is not destroyed but retained pursuant to applicable laws even after the retention period has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, it must be managed so that only the minimum information is retained, limited to the minimum period prescribed by the applicable laws.

- Limit the personal data items to the minimum items appropriate for the retention purpose
- Set the retention period to the minimum period prescribed by the applicable laws

  Note: Retention periods under other laws (examples)
  - Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, etc.
    (1) Records on labeling/advertising : 6 months
    (2) Records on contracts or withdrawal of subscription, etc. : 5 years
    (3) Records on payment and supply of goods, etc. : 5 years
    (4) Records on consumer complaints or dispute resolution : 3 years
  - Protection of Communications Secrets Act
    Log records of computer communications or the internet, and access location tracking data that can confirm the location of information and communications devices : 3 months

Where personal data is not destroyed but retained pursuant to applicable laws even after the retention period has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, that personal data or personal data file must be stored/managed separately from other personal data.

- Configure the separate database as physically or logically separated

The separately stored personal data must be managed so that it can be processed only within the scope of the purpose prescribed by law.

- Prohibit the use of separately stored personal data for other purposes such as marketing

Access authority to the separately stored personal data must be restricted to the minimum number of personnel.

- Minimize access authority, such as restricting access rights to the separate database to the minimum number of personnel
- Keep access logs for the separate database and review them regularly, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 21 (Destruction of Personal Information)

## Evidence (examples)

- Regulations on personal data retention period and destruction
- Status of the separate database (table structure, etc.)
- Status of access authority to the separate database

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where withdrawn members' information is not destroyed but retained for a certain period under the Electronic Commerce Act, yet is stored in the same table as other members' information with only a flag value changed
- A case where, for records on consumer complaints and dispute resolution under the Electronic Commerce Act, the applicable legal requirement is misapplied so that retention is set to 5 years instead of 3 years
- A case where a separate database is configured but access authority is not set separately, so that personnel who do not need access for their work can also freely access the separate database
- A case where withdrawn members' information is not destroyed but records on contracts or withdrawal of subscription and on payment and supply of goods are retained separately under the Electronic Commerce Act, yet even optional information for which there is no retention obligation under the Electronic Commerce Act is excessively retained
