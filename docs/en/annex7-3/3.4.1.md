## Certification criterion

An internal policy on the retention period and destruction of personal information shall be established, and when the point of destruction has arrived, such as the expiration of the retention period or the achievement of the processing purpose, the personal information shall be destroyed without delay in a manner that ensures the safety and completeness of the destruction.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is an internal policy on the retention period and destruction of personal information established?
2. When the processing purpose of the personal information has been achieved or the retention period has expired, is the relevant personal information destroyed without delay?
3. When personal information is destroyed, is it destroyed by a safe method so that it cannot be recovered/reproduced?
4. Are records of the destruction of personal information kept and managed?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special cases for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the officially relaxed/adjusted text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.4.1 Destruction of Personal Information] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

An internal policy on the retention period and destruction of personal information shall be established, including the following matters.

- For each collected item, each collection purpose, and each collection channel: the storage location (database, backup data, etc.), destruction method, destruction timing, statutory basis, etc.
- Public institutions shall establish and implement a personal information destruction plan reflecting the retention period, processing purpose, etc. of the personal information file, and the personal information destruction plan may be included in the internal management plan (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines Article 55, Paragraph 2)

When the processing purpose of the personal information has been achieved or the retention period has expired, the relevant personal information shall be destroyed without delay.

- When the personal information has become unnecessary, such as the achievement of the processing purpose, the abolition of the relevant service, the termination of the business, or the expiration of the statutory retention period, the personal information shall be destroyed within 5 days therefrom unless there is a justifiable reason

  Note: Cases where the purpose of collecting and using personal information has been achieved (examples)
  / Where the data subject has withdrawn from membership of the website
  / Where the data subject has terminated high-speed internet service
  / Where the data subject has requested withdrawal from mileage membership
  / Where the event that collects personal information has ended
  / Where, after providing information to a third-party company for telemarketing, the TM (telemarketing) work of that company has ended, etc.

When personal information is destroyed, it shall be destroyed by a safe method so that it cannot be recovered/reproduced. Here, a method by which recovery is impossible means a method of taking measures so that, at the current level of technology and at a cost reasonable under generally accepted social norms, recovery of the destroyed personal information is impossible.

- Complete destruction (incineration/shredding, etc.)
- Deletion using dedicated demagnetizing equipment (equipment that deletes data on storage devices using a magnetic field)
- Performing initialization or overwriting so that data cannot be recovered

  Note: Destruction methods (examples)
  / Complete destruction: destroying paper documents such as membership application forms containing personal information, or hard disks or magnetic tapes, by using a shredder, dissolving them, or burning them at an incineration site or incinerator, etc.
  / When using dedicated demagnetizing equipment: deleting personal information stored on hard disks or magnetic tapes using a degausser, etc.
  / When performing initialization or overwriting so that data cannot be recovered: methods such as performing a complete format on the hard disk storing personal information (3 or more times recommended), overwriting the data area with random values, 0, 1, etc. (3 or more times recommended), or encrypting and storing the relevant drive with a secure algorithm and key length and then deleting it and completely disposing of the key used for encryption and overwriting it with random values, etc.

- Where only part of the personal information is destroyed, when it is difficult to destroy it by the above methods, the following measures shall be implemented
  1. In the case of an electronic file form: manage and supervise so that the personal information cannot be recovered or reproduced after deletion
  2. In the case of records, printed matter, written documents, or other recording media other than an electronic file form: delete the relevant part by masking, punching holes, etc.
- Where it is markedly difficult to destroy the personal information by the above methods due to technical characteristics, take measures to make recovery impossible by processing it into information falling under Article 58-2 of the Act (anonymization)

Records of the destruction of personal information shall be kept and managed.

- The implementation of personal information destruction and the confirmation of destruction results shall be performed under the responsibility of the personal information protection officer, and matters concerning destruction shall be recorded/managed
- Records of personal information destruction shall be recorded in a destruction management ledger, or photographs, etc. containing the destruction details shall be kept as records
- When a public institution destroys a personal information file, it shall confirm the destruction results and prepare a personal information file destruction management ledger (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines Article 55)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 21 (Destruction of Personal Information)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 13 (Destruction of Personal Information)

## Evidence (examples)

- Regulations on the retention period and destruction of personal information
- Personal information destruction results (member database, etc.)
- Personal information destruction management ledger

## Nonconformity examples

- Where, when a purpose has been achieved such as membership withdrawal or the retention period has expired, the relevant personal information was destroyed from the member database, but the personal information replicated and stored in linked personal information processing systems such as CRM/DW was not destroyed
- Where, for personal information collected during an event conducted for a specific period, destruction criteria were not established or destruction has not been carried out even after the event ended
- Where personal information related to complaint handling collected at a call center (consultation history, recordings, etc.) is retained for 3 years based on the Electronic Commerce Act, but is kept without being destroyed even after 3 years have elapsed
- Where, because complete destruction of personal information whose purpose has been achieved is difficult due to technical characteristics such as blockchain, anonymization was performed instead of complete destruction, but the anonymization was not performed properly, so that recovery such as re-identification of some personal information is possible
