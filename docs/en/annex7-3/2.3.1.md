## Certification criterion

When using external services or outsourcing business operations to external parties, the resulting information security and personal information protection requirements must be identified, and the relevant contents must be specified in the contract or agreement.

## Key checkpoints

1. When selecting external services and outsourcing vendors related to the processing of important information and personal information, are procedures in place to take information security and personal information protection capabilities into account?
2. Are the information security and personal information protection requirements arising from the use of external services and the outsourcing of business operations identified and specified in the contract or agreement?
3. When outsourcing the development of information systems and personal information processing systems, are the information security and personal information protection requirements to be complied with during development specified in the contract?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [2.3.2 Security in External Party Contracts] (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

When selecting external services and outsourcing vendors related to the processing of important information and personal information, procedures must be established to take information security and personal information protection capabilities into account.

- Reflect the relevant requirements in the request for proposal (RFP) and proposal evaluation items so that a vendor with information security and personal information protection capabilities can be selected, and apply them when selecting the vendor

When outsourcing the organization's information processing operations to external parties or using external services, the following security requirements must be defined and reflected in the contract.

- Compliance with laws related to information security and personal information protection, submission of an information security and personal information protection pledge
- Periodic information security training for employees performing the outsourced operations and periodic security inspections
- Measures to prevent leakage of important information obtained in the course of performing the operations
- Restriction of external parties' internet access, physical protection measures (bringing in/out equipment and media, etc.), terminal security for PCs, etc. (installing anti-virus software, setting safe passwords and changing them periodically, setting screen savers, etc.), restriction on the use of wireless networks
- Procedures for granting and revoking access rights so that excessive privileges are not granted when information system access is permitted
- Restriction of re-outsourcing and definition of procedures and security requirements when re-outsourcing is necessary
- Penalties for violation of security requirements, liability for damages, obligation to report in the event of a security incident, etc.

  Note: Matters to be included in the document when outsourcing personal information processing operations (Article 26(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act and Article 28(1) of its Enforcement Decree)
  - Matters regarding the prohibition of processing personal information for purposes other than performing the outsourced operations
  - Matters regarding technical/administrative protection measures for personal information
  - The purpose and scope of the outsourced operations
  - Matters regarding restriction of re-outsourcing
  - Matters regarding measures to ensure safety, such as restricting access to personal information
  - Matters regarding supervision, such as inspecting the management status of personal information held in connection with the outsourced operations
  - Matters regarding liability, such as damages, in the event the trustee violates the obligations it must comply with

When outsourcing the development of information systems and personal information processing systems, the information security and personal information protection requirements to be complied with during development must be specified in the contract.

- Compliance with legal requirements related to information security and personal information protection
- Application of development security procedures, such as compliance with secure coding standards
- Vulnerability inspection and remediation for the completed information systems and personal information processing systems
- Security management of the development environment, including development-related deliverables, source programs, and development data
- Obligation to maintain confidentiality of information obtained during the development process
- Matters regarding liability, such as damages, in the event of a violation, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 26 (Restrictions on Processing of Personal Information Following Outsourcing of Operations)

## Evidence (examples)

- Outsourcing contract
- Information security and personal information protection agreement (covenant, supplementary agreement)
- Internal guidelines related to outsourcing
- RFP (request for proposal) and evaluation sheet related to vendor selection

## Nonconformity examples

- Where no outsourcing contract exists for an outsourcing service provider entrusted with IT operations, development, and personal information processing operations
- Where the outsourcing contract with an external vendor entrusted with personal information processing operations does not include some items required by laws such as the Personal Information Protection Act (matters regarding management/supervision, etc.)
- Where infrastructure operations and part of the personal information processing operations are outsourced to an external vendor, but the contract, etc., does not identify/reflect the security requirements appropriate to the characteristics of the outsourced operations and stipulates only general matters regarding confidentiality and damages
