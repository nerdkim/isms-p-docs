## Certification criterion

When providing personal information to a third party, it must be based on a legal basis or with the consent of the data subject, and protective measures must be established/implemented to safely protect personal information during the provision process, including where a third party is allowed access to personal information.

## Key checkpoints

1. When providing personal information to a third party, are the lawful requirements, such as consent of the data subject and compliance with statutory obligations, clearly identified and complied with?
2. When obtaining consent from the data subject for the provision of personal information to a third party, are the relevant matters clearly notified and consent obtained lawfully, separately from other consent items?
3. When obtaining consent from the data subject for the provision of personal information to a third party, are the relevant contents clearly notified and the important matters prescribed by law clearly indicated so that they are easy to recognize?
4. When providing personal information to a third party, is it limited to the minimum personal information items appropriate to the purpose of provision?
5. When providing personal information to a third party, is it provided through safe procedures and methods, and are the records of provision kept and retained?
6. When a third party is allowed access to personal information, is it controlled in accordance with protective procedures to safely protect personal information?
7. When personal information is additionally provided without the consent of the data subject, are judgment criteria for the considerations such as relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether it unfairly infringes interests, and measures for ensuring safety established/implemented, and where additional provision occurs continuously, are the judgment criteria for the considerations disclosed in the privacy policy and checked?

## Detailed explanation

> ※ This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the officially relaxed/adjusted original text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the contents of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.3.1 Provision of Personal Information to Third Parties] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

When providing personal information to a third party, the lawful requirements under relevant laws, such as consent of the data subject and compliance with statutory obligations, must be clearly identified, and personal information must be provided lawfully accordingly.

- **Scope of third party**
  - All parties except the data subject and the personal information controller that collects/holds personal information about the data subject (other departments and organizations within the same personal information controller do not constitute a third party)

  ※ Provision of personal information to third parties (examples)
  / Physically transferring a personal information storage medium or printouts/booklets containing personal information
  / Transmitting personal information over a network
  / Granting a third party access rights to personal information
  / Sharing personal information between the personal information controller and a third party
  / Any other act that results in the transfer or joint use of personal information

- Clearly identify the lawful requirements for provision of personal information for each provision channel, and record/manage the relevant grounds so that they can be substantiated
  - For example, where personal information is provided without the consent of the data subject because there is a special provision in the law or to comply with a statutory obligation, document the relevant grounds such as the provision of the applicable law or statute

- A personal information controller may provide (including sharing) personal information to a third party in any of the following cases

  **Cases where provision of personal information to a third party is permitted (Article 17(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. Where the consent of the data subject is obtained
  2. Where there is a special provision in the law or where unavoidable to comply with a statutory obligation
  3. Where unavoidable for a public institution to perform its duties within its jurisdiction as prescribed by statutes, etc.
  4. Where deemed manifestly necessary for the urgent life, body, or property interests of the data subject or a third party
  5. Where necessary to attain the legitimate interest of the personal information controller and such interest is manifestly superior to the rights of the data subject. In this case, it is limited to cases where there is substantial relevance to the legitimate interest of the personal information controller and it does not exceed a reasonable scope.
  6. Where urgently necessary for public safety and security, such as public health

  ※ Under items 2 through 6 above, personal information may be provided without the consent of the data subject within the scope of the purpose for which the personal information was collected

Consent for the provision of personal information to a third party must be obtained separately from consent for collection/use, etc., and unless the provision to a third party is essential to perform the fundamental function of the service, the provision of the service must not be refused on the grounds that consent to it is not given.

  **Cases where consent items must be distinguished (Article 22(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. Where consent is obtained under Article 15(1)1 (consent to collection/use of personal information)
  2. Where consent is obtained under Article 17(1)1 (consent to provision of personal information to a third party)
  3. Where consent is obtained under Article 18(2)1 (consent to use/provision of personal information beyond the purpose)
  4. Where consent is obtained under Article 19(1) (consent to use/provision beyond the purpose by a party who received personal information)
  5. Where consent is obtained under Article 24(1)1 (consent to processing of sensitive information)
  6. Where consent is obtained under Article 24(1)1 (consent to processing of unique identifying information)
  7. Where consent to the processing of personal information is sought in order to promote or solicit the sale of goods or services

  ※ Indicate distinctly so that the data subject can clearly know that they may choose whether to give consent

When obtaining consent from the data subject for the provision of personal information to a third party, the relevant contents must be clearly notified and the important matters prescribed by law clearly indicated so that they are easy to recognize.

- When obtaining consent from the data subject for the provision of personal information to a third party, the five statutory matters to be notified must be informed specifically and clearly, and consent must be obtained

  **Matters to be notified when obtaining consent for provision of personal information to a third party (Article 17(2) of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. The recipient of the personal information
  2. The purpose of the recipient's use of the personal information
  3. The items of personal information to be provided
  4. The period for which the recipient will retain and use the personal information
  5. The fact that the data subject has the right to refuse consent, and where there is a disadvantage from refusing consent, the details of such disadvantage

- For the consent of the data subject to be lawful, all lawful requirements must be met, such as the data subject deciding whether to consent according to their free will, and the specificity and clarity of the consent content

  **Conditions to be met when obtaining consent from the data subject (Article 17(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. The data subject can decide whether to consent according to their free will
  2. The content for which consent is sought is specific and clear
  3. Phrasing that is easy to read and understand is used
  4. A method for the data subject to clearly indicate whether they consent is provided

  ※ However, this provision takes effect from September 15, 2024

- Under Article 22(2) (methods of obtaining consent) of the Personal Information Protection Act, when consent to the processing of personal information is obtained in writing (including electronic documents under Article 2(1) of the Framework Act on Electronic Documents and Electronic Transactions), the important contents must be clearly indicated so that they are easy to recognize, as follows

  **Important contents that must be clearly indicated (Article 17(3) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  / The fact that, among the purposes of collection/use of personal information, the personal information may be used to contact the data subject for promoting or soliciting the sale of goods or services
  / Among the personal information items processed, sensitive information, passport number, driver's license number, and alien registration number
  / The retention and use period of the personal information (in the case of provision, the retention and use period of the recipient)
  / The recipient of the personal information and the purpose of the recipient's use of the personal information

  **Method of indicating important contents (Article 4 of the Notice on Personal Information Processing Methods)**
  / Ensure that the content is clearly indicated through font size, color, boldness, underlining, etc.
  / Where the consent items are numerous and it is difficult to clearly distinguish the important contents, indicate the important contents separately from other contents so that they can be easily identified

  ※ For details, refer to the "Easy-to-Understand Guide to Consent for Personal Information Processing (Personal Information Protection Commission)"

When providing personal information to a third party, it must be limited to the minimum personal information items appropriate to the purpose of provision.

- For provision to a third party based on consent: Only the minimum personal information items necessary to achieve the provision purpose notified at the time of consent must be provided
- For provision to a third party based on statute: Only the minimum personal information items must be provided within the scope specifically stated in the law or necessary to comply with the applicable statutory obligation

In the process of providing personal information to a third party, it must be provided through safe procedures and methods so that personal information is not leaked/exposed, and the relevant records of provision must be kept and retained.

  ※ Safe procedures for provision to a third party (examples)
  / Clarifying the responsibility relationship for ensuring safety between the party providing and the party receiving personal information (contracts, etc.)
  / Approval procedures related to third-party provision (when provided by a person in charge)
  / Encryption during transmission or delivery
  / Application of safety measures such as access control and access rights management
  / Preservation of provision records, etc.

  ※ Contents to be included in third-party provision records (examples)
  / Recipient
  / Date and time of provision
  / Personal information provided: identification information of the data subject and personal information items
  / Purpose or grounds of provision
  / Provider (person in charge): including the approver where there is an approval procedure
  / Method of provision: system interlinking, email transmission, etc.
  / Other necessary information

When a third party is allowed access to personal information, it must be controlled in accordance with protective procedures to safely protect personal information.

- Safe authentication and access control measures so that only authorized persons can access
- Encryption measures to prevent eavesdropping in the transmission section
- Preservation of access logs to ensure accountability, etc.

When personal information is additionally provided without the consent of the data subject, judgment criteria for considerations such as relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether it infringes interests, and measures for ensuring safety must be established/implemented, and where additional provision occurs continuously, this must be disclosed in the privacy policy and compliance with the criteria checked.

  **Considerations for additional provision of personal information (Article 14-2(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. Whether there is relevance to the original collection purpose
  2. Whether there is foreseeability of additional use or provision of personal information in light of the circumstances of collection or the processing practices
  3. Whether it unfairly infringes the interests of the data subject
  4. Whether measures necessary for ensuring safety, such as pseudonymization or encryption, have been taken

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 17 (Provision of Personal Information), Article 22 (Methods of Obtaining Consent)
- Notice on Personal Information Processing Methods

## Evidence (examples)

- Online forms related to provision of personal information to third parties (website membership registration screen, consent screen for provision of personal information to third parties, etc.)
- Offline forms related to provision of personal information to third parties (membership registration application, consent form for provision of personal information to third parties, etc.)
- Records of third-party provision
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Where the personal information controller omits some of the matters to be notified to the data subject (right to refuse consent, items to be provided, etc.) when obtaining consent for provision of personal information to a third party
- Where, in the process of providing personal information to a third party, whether consent to third-party provision was given is not properly verified, so that the personal information of a data subject who did not consent is provided together
- Where consent for provision of personal information is obtained by generically informing the data subject without specifying the recipient, such as with "~ etc."
- Where consent for third-party provision is obtained as an optional item at the membership registration stage, but the membership registration process cannot proceed further if consent to third-party provision is not given
- Where excessively large amounts of personal information are provided regardless of the recipient's purpose of use
