## Certification criterion

User accounts shall be assigned an identifier that uniquely distinguishes each user, and the use of guessable identifiers shall be restricted. Where the same identifier is shared and used, the reason and its validity shall be reviewed, and supplementary measures such as obtaining the responsible person's approval and ensuring accountability (traceability) shall be established and implemented.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are unique identifiers that can uniquely distinguish users and personal information handlers assigned in information systems and personal information processing systems, and is the use of guessable identifiers restricted?
2. Where the same identifier is unavoidably shared and used, is the reason and its validity reviewed, supplementary measures prepared, and the responsible person's approval obtained?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special provision for information security management system certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the officially relaxed/adjusted text, while the detailed explanation, related laws, evidence, and nonconformity examples below are drawn from the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, 2.5.2 User Identification in Annex 7 (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

When registering users for information systems and personal information processing systems, an identifier that can uniquely distinguish each user and personal information handler shall be assigned, and the use of guessable identifiers shall be restricted.

- Ensure user accountability (traceability) by adopting the principle of issuing one account per person
- Restrict account sharing and the use of shared (common) accounts
- Restrict general users' access to the operating accounts used by the system
- After system installation, remove the default and test accounts provided by the manufacturer or vendor, or change them to accounts that are hard to guess before use (including changing default passwords)
- Restrict the use of easily guessable identifiers (root, admin, administrator, etc.) for administrator and special-privilege accounts

Where the same identifier is unavoidably shared and used for business reasons, the reason and its validity shall be reviewed and the responsible person's approval obtained.

- Even when an administrator account is shared because primary/secondary roles are separated in the division of duties, assign a separate user account and switch to the administrator account after logging in with the user account
- Where an account has been temporarily shared for tasks such as maintenance, change that account's password immediately after the work is completed
- Where the use of a shared (common) account is unavoidably necessary for business reasons, review the reason and its validity, obtain the responsible person's approval, and apply additional controls to guarantee accountability (traceability)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 5 (Management of Access Rights)

## Evidence (examples)

- Login screens of information systems and personal information processing systems
- Account lists of administrators, users, and personal information handlers of information systems and personal information processing systems
- Approval records for exception handling

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, upon checking the account status of information systems (servers, network, firewalls, DBMS, etc.), the default administrator account provided by the manufacturer is being used without change even though it can be technically changed
- A case where a developer is using a personal information processing system account in common, but is using it without a validity review or the responsible person's approval
- A case where the operating account of an information system maintained by external staff is being used like a personal account without a separate approval procedure
