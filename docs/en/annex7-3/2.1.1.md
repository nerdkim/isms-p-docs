## Certification criterion

Information security and personal information protection policies and their implementing documents shall be reviewed periodically in accordance with laws and regulations, alignment with the policies of higher-level organizations and relevant agencies, changes in the organization's internal and external environment, and so on, and shall be enacted/revised where necessary, with the history of such changes maintained.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are procedures for periodic validity review of information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents established and implemented?
2. When significant changes occur in the organization's internal and external environment, is the impact on information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents reviewed, and are they enacted/revised where necessary?
3. When information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents are enacted/revised, is the review of stakeholders obtained?
4. Is the history of enactment/revision of information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents maintained?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special case of Information Security Management System certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [2.1.1 Maintenance of Policies] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

Procedures for periodic validity review of information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents (guidelines, procedures, guidance documents, etc.) shall be established and implemented, and where necessary the relevant policies and implementing documents shall be enacted/revised.

- Establish periodic validity review procedures for information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents
  - Note: matters that should be included in the periodic validity review procedure (examples)
    - Review cycle and timing: review required at least once a year
    - Roles and responsibilities of each relevant organization
    - Responsible department and person in charge
    - Review method
    - Follow-up action procedures: where enactment/revision of policies and implementing documents is required, the relevant procedures, internal consultation and reporting procedures, etc.
- Perform validity review taking the following into account so that laws and regulations, alignment with the policies of higher-level organizations and relevant agencies, changes in the organization's internal and external environment, and so on can be reflected
  - Analyze alignment with the information security and personal information protection policies of higher-level organizations and relevant agencies, and review whether there are elements that are mutually inconsistent and whether the hierarchical structure among policies is appropriate
  - Review whether consistency is maintained across documents in terms of the cycle, level, and method of information security and personal information protection activities
  - Review whether enactments/revisions (including planned ones) of information security and personal information protection laws and regulations have occurred and whether such matters are appropriately reflected in the policies and implementing documents
  - Reflect the results of risk assessment and management system reviews
  - Reflect changes in the IT environment such as the discovery of new threats and vulnerabilities, changes in the business environment, and the introduction of new technologies, as well as changes in the information security and personal information protection environment

When significant changes occur in the organization's internal and external environment as follows, the impact on information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents shall be reviewed and they shall be enacted/revised where necessary.

- Enactment/revision of information security and personal information protection laws and regulations
- Changes in the business environment (entry into new business areas, large-scale organizational restructuring, etc.)
- Significant changes in the information security, personal information protection, and IT environment (introduction of new security systems or IT systems, etc.)
- Occurrence of a significant internal/external security incident
- Discovery of new threats or vulnerabilities, etc.

When enacting/revising information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents, the relevant content shall be sufficiently consulted on and reviewed with stakeholders.

- Identify and consult with stakeholders such as the Chief Information Security Officer and the Chief Privacy Officer, information security and personal information protection organizations, the IT department, departments handling important information and personal information, and important information handlers and personal information handlers
- Consider the impact on operations and legal compliance resulting from changes to information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents
- Retain evidence of reviewed matters such as meeting minutes, and reflect the relevant matters in policies/guidelines, etc.

To record and manage the history of changes (enactment, revision, distribution, disposal, etc.) to information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents, a document management procedure shall be established and implemented.

- Record and manage revision history within the document, including document version, date, reason for revision, author, and approver
- Distribute and manage documents so that relevant executives and employees can always refer to the latest version

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 4 (Establishment/Implementation and Inspection of the Internal Management Plan)

## Evidence (examples)

- Information security and personal information protection policies and implementing documents (guidelines, procedures, guides, manuals, etc.)
- Results of periodic/ad hoc validity reviews of policies/guidelines
- Meeting minutes and circulated materials of reviews with policy/guideline-related departments
- Enactment/revision history of policies/guidelines

## Nonconformity examples

- The password setting rules are inconsistent between the guideline document and the procedure document
- The target, cycle, level, method, etc. of information security activities (information security training, encryption, backup, etc.) are specified differently across the relevant internal regulations, guidelines, and procedures, resulting in a lack of consistency
- A database access control solution has been newly introduced and operated to effectively record and manage access to and operation history of the database, but matters concerning access control, operation history, logging, review, etc. are not reflected in the internal security guidelines such as the security system security management guideline and the database security management guideline
- The personal information protection policy was revised, but the effective date of the policy is not specified, and the preparation date, author, and approver of the relevant policy are omitted
- Significant changes occurred in personal information protection laws, public notices, etc., but the impact of such changes on the personal information protection policy and implementing documents was not reviewed, or they were not revised to reflect the changes
