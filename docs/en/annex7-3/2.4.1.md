# 2.4.1 Access Control  _( Annex 7-3 )_

## Certification criterion

Controlled areas shall block access by unauthorized persons, and to ensure accountability, entry and access history shall be reviewed periodically.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are controlled areas managed so that only persons authorized under the access procedure may enter?
2. Are internal/external entry records for each controlled area retained for a certain period, and are entry records and access privileges reviewed periodically?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are drawn from the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [2.4.2 Access Control] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

For each protected area, internal/external access control procedures shall be established so that only authorized persons may enter, and the status of persons permitted to enter shall be managed.

- Define the departments/roles/tasks permitted to enter each protected area, identify the employees granted access privileges, and manage their status
- For controlled areas, restrict entry to the minimum number of persons according to business purpose
- Access procedure: entry application, approval by the responsible person, granting and revocation of access privileges, recording of entry details, periodic review of entry records, etc.
- Installation of access control devices: password-based, ID card-based, biometric-based, etc.
- Establishment/operation of access control procedures: registration/deletion of persons entering, management of access privileges, visitor management, entry logbook management, etc.

Internal/external entry records for each protected area shall be retained for a certain period, and entry records and access privileges shall be reviewed periodically.

- Retain entry records for a certain period in documentary or electronic form so that post-hoc monitoring is possible
- Review of entry records and access privileges: long-term non-entrants, abnormal entry attempts, whether access privileges are over-granted, etc.
- Identify unauthorized entry attempts, long-term non-entrants, etc., verify the reasons, and take action
- Through periodic review, recover access passes from and delete access privileges of departing employees, and adjust access privileges upon job changes
- Where entry logs cannot be recorded by the system, prepare an entry logbook to verify entry records

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 10 (Physical Safety Measures)

## Evidence (examples)

- Access management logbook and access logs
- Access registration application and approval records
- Entry record review report
- Access control system management screen (registration status of persons entering, etc.)

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where controlled areas are defined, protection measures are established, and employees permitted to enter are managed, but entry records are not reviewed periodically, so many long-term non-entrants exist due to retirement, transfer, etc.
- A case where access control devices are installed in controlled areas such as the computer room and document storage room, but are kept open for long periods without a valid reason or approval
- A case where some external partner company employees are granted access cards allowing excessive, permanent entry to all areas
