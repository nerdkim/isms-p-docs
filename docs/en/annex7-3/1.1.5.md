## Certification criterion

The organization shall establish/prepare information protection policies and implementation documents, and in doing so shall clearly present the organization's information protection principles and direction. In addition, the policies and implementation documents shall receive management approval and be delivered to executives, employees, and relevant parties in a form that is easy to understand.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is a top-level information protection policy established that includes the basis for all information protection activities carried out by the organization?
2. Are guidelines, procedures, manuals, and the like established that specify the detailed methods, procedures, cycles, and other matters necessary to implement the information protection policy?
3. When information protection policies/implementation documents are enacted/revised, is approval obtained from the chief executive officer or from a person delegated authority by the chief executive officer?
4. Are the latest versions of the information protection policies/implementation documents provided to relevant executives and employees in a form that is easy to access?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special provisions for information protection management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, and the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [1.1.5 Policy Establishment], which is in the same control domain (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

The top-level information protection and personal information protection policy that includes the basis for all information protection and personal information protection activities carried out by the organization shall be established so as to include the following content.

- The will and direction of management, including the chief executive officer, regarding the organization's information protection and personal information protection
- The roles/responsibilities and targets/scope for the organization's information protection and personal information protection
- The basis for the administrative/technical/physical information protection and personal information protection activities carried out by the organization

To specifically implement the information protection and personal information protection matters specified in the information protection and personal information protection policy, subordinate execution documents such as guidelines, procedures, manuals, and guides that specify the necessary detailed methods, procedures, cycles, and responsible parties shall be established in a manner suited to the organization's characteristics.

- The subordinate execution documents shall be able to specifically present the basis for all information protection and personal information protection activities carried out by the organization, and shall be established to suit the organization's characteristics from various perspectives, such as the protection-target perspective or the responsible-party perspective
  - Note: subordinate execution documents (examples)

  | Protection-target perspective | Responsible-party perspective |
  | --- | --- |
  | Server security guideline | Executive/employee security guideline |
  | Network security guideline | Developer security guideline |
  | Database security guideline | Operator security guideline, etc. |
  | Application security guideline | |
  | Web service security guideline | |
  | Cloud security guideline | |

- The policies and implementation documents (guidelines, procedures, etc.) shall reflect the personal information protection legal requirements (statutes, enforcement decrees, enforcement rules, subordinate notices, guides, etc.) related to the services, businesses, and the like provided by the organization
- Where personal information is processed, an internal management plan under the Personal Information Protection Act shall be established, including all matters required by the related laws and regulations
  - Matters that must be included in the internal management plan under the Personal Information Protection Act
    1. Matters concerning the composition and operation of the personal information protection organization
    2. Matters concerning the qualification requirements for, and the designation of, the personal information protection officer
    3. Matters concerning the roles and responsibilities of the personal information protection officer and personal information handlers
    4. Matters concerning the management/supervision and education of personal information handlers
    5. Matters concerning the management of access rights
    6. Matters concerning access control
    7. Matters concerning the encryption of personal information
    8. Matters concerning the retention and inspection of access records
    9. Matters concerning the prevention of malicious programs, etc.
    10. Matters concerning vulnerability inspection to prevent the leakage, theft, etc. of personal information
    11. Matters concerning physical safeguards
    12. Matters concerning the establishment/implementation of a response plan for personal information leakage incidents
    13. Matters concerning risk analysis and management
    14. Matters concerning the management and supervision of trustees where personal information processing work is entrusted
    15. Matters concerning the establishment, modification, and approval of the internal management plan for personal information
    16. Other matters necessary for the protection of personal information
    - Note: however, for small business owners/individuals/organizations that process personal information concerning fewer than 10,000 data subjects, this may be omitted

When information protection and personal information protection policies/implementation documents are enacted/revised, approval shall be obtained from the chief executive officer or from a person delegated authority by the chief executive officer.

- When enacting/revising the policy document and implementation documents, sufficiently consult/review the relevant content with stakeholders
- Consider the impact of changes to the policy document and implementation documents on the organization's work and services, legal compliance, and the like
- Keep records of the reviewed matters, such as meeting minutes, and reflect the relevant matters in the policies/guidelines, etc.
- Report the reviewed policy document and implementation documents to management and obtain approval

When information protection and personal information protection policies/implementation documents are enacted/revised, the latest version shall be provided to relevant executives and employees in a form that is easy to understand.

- Provide it in a form that executives, employees, and external parties can easily refer to (electronic bulletin board, booklet, educational materials, manuals, etc.)
- Whenever enactment/revision of the policy document and implementation documents occurs, announce it immediately and maintain the latest version

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Obligation of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Secure the Safety of Personal Information Article 4 (Establishment/Implementation and Inspection of the Internal Management Plan)

## Evidence (examples)

- Information protection and personal information protection policies/guidelines/procedure documents (including enactment/revision history)
- Meeting minutes of stakeholder reviews at the time of enacting/revising information protection and personal information protection policies/guideline procedure documents
- Internal management plan for personal information
- Notice records of the enactment/revision of information protection and personal information protection policies/guidelines (groupware, in-house bulletin board, etc.)
- Meeting minutes of the information protection and personal information protection committee

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, although internal regulations require that the enactment/revision of information protection and personal information protection policy documents go through a resolution of the information protection and personal information protection committee, a recent revision of the policy document was made based solely on the approval of the Chief Information Security Officer and the personal information protection officer, without submitting it as an agenda item to the committee
- A case where the information protection and personal information protection policies and guidelines were recently revised, but the relevant matters were not shared/delivered to the relevant departments and executives/employees, so that some departments are performing their work based on the old version of the guidelines
- A case where the information protection and personal information protection policies and guidelines are managed only by the security department and are not provided in a form such as a bulletin board or document so that executives and employees can view them
