## Certification criterion

To protect personal information and critical information, the organization shall establish encryption targets, cryptographic strength, cryptographic use policies, and encryption key management procedures that reflect legal requirements, and shall apply encryption when storing/transmitting/delivering personal information and critical information.

## Key checkpoints

1. To protect personal information and critical information, has a cryptography policy been established that reflects legal requirements and includes encryption targets, cryptographic strength, cryptographic use, and so forth?
2. In accordance with the cryptography policy, is encryption performed when storing, transmitting, and delivering personal information and critical information?
3. Have procedures for encryption key generation, use, storage, distribution, change, recovery, and destruction been established and implemented?
4. Are encryption keys kept in a separate secure location so that recovery is possible when needed, and are access rights to the use of encryption keys minimized?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the official relaxed/adjusted text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are from the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [2.7.1 Application of Cryptography Policy] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

To protect personal information and critical information, a cryptography policy shall be established that reflects legal requirements and includes encryption targets, cryptographic strength, cryptographic use, and so forth.

- Encryption targets: defined according to legal requirements and the sensitivity and importance of the processed information

**Personal information subject to encryption under the Personal Information Protection Act**

| Field | | Personal information of data subjects who are not users | Personal information of users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When transmitting/receiving via an information and communications network | Information and communications network, internet network | - | Authentication information (passwords, biometric information, etc.), personal information |
| When storing | Regardless of storage location | - | Authentication information (passwords, biometric information, etc.) Note: passwords are one-way encrypted / resident registration numbers Note: encrypted pursuant to Article 24-2 (2) of the Act |
| When storing | Internet section, DMZ | Unique identifying information | Resident registration numbers, passport numbers, driver's license numbers, alien registration numbers, credit card numbers, account numbers, biometric information Note: regardless of storage location |
| When storing | Internal network | Note: however, where unique identifying information other than resident registration numbers is stored on the internal network, whether and to what extent encryption is applied may be determined and implemented according to the results of a privacy impact assessment or a risk analysis | (same as above) |
| When storing on a personal information handler's computer, mobile device, auxiliary storage media, etc. | | Unique identifying information, biometric information | Personal information |

Note: however, regarding authentication information (passwords, biometric information, etc.) when transmitting/receiving via an information and communications network, for those subject to the former standards for measures to ensure the safety of personal information, this takes effect on 2024.9.15.

- Encryption algorithm: select a secure encryption algorithm and security strength considering legal requirements and so forth

Note: secure encryption algorithms (examples) (refer to the "Guide to Encryption Measures for Personal Information")

| Field | Algorithm name |
| --- | --- |
| Symmetric key encryption algorithms | SEED, ARIA-128/192/256, AES-128/192/256, HIGHT, LEA, etc. |
| Public key encryption algorithms | RSAES-OAEP, etc. |
| One-way encryption algorithms | SHA-256/384/512, etc. |

In accordance with the cryptography policy, encryption shall be performed when storing, transmitting, and delivering personal information and critical information.

- Select and apply an encryption method considering the encryption location, system characteristics, and so forth

Note: encryption methods (examples)

| Field | Encryption method |
| --- | --- |
| When transmitting/receiving via an information and communications network | 1. Install an SSL (Secure Socket Layer) certificate on the web server to encrypt the information being transmitted for secure transmission/reception<br>2. Install an encryption application on the web server to encrypt the information being transmitted for transmission/reception<br>3. Use of other encryption technologies: VPN, PGP, etc. |
| When storing in a personal information processing system, etc. | 1. Application-level self-encryption (API method)<br>2. Database server encryption (Plug-in method)<br>3. DBMS self-encryption (TDE method)<br>4. Calling DBMS encryption functions<br>5. Operating system encryption (file encryption, etc.)<br>6. Use of other encryption technologies |
| When storing on business computers and mobile devices | 1. Document tool self-encryption (using the password setting function provided by office applications, etc.)<br>2. Encryption using a cryptographic utility<br>3. Application of DRM (Digital Rights Management) technology, etc. |
| When storing on auxiliary storage media | 1. Use of secure storage media that provide encryption functions (secure USB, etc.)<br>2. Encrypting the relevant information and then storing it on auxiliary storage media, etc. |

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 24-2 (Restrictions on Processing Resident Registration Numbers), Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 7 (Encryption of Personal Information)

## Evidence (examples)

- Cryptographic control policy (targets, methods, algorithms, etc.)
- Encryption application status (when storing and transmitting)
- Risk analysis results (when encryption is not applied to unique identifying information other than resident registration numbers on the internal network)
- Encryption solution management screens

## Nonconformity examples

- Where internal policies/guidelines do not appropriately specify matters such as encryption targets reflecting legal requirements for cryptographic control, cryptographic strength, encryption methods for storage and transmission, and the roles and responsibilities of personnel in charge of encryption
- Where, in establishing the cryptography policy, the organization incorrectly applies the laws to which it is subject and fails to comply with legal requirements related to encryption (for example, storing a user's account number without applying encryption)
- Where one-way encryption is applied to the passwords of personal information handlers and data subjects, but the insecure MD5 algorithm is used
- Where a personal information controller applied a secure server to an internet shopping mall in accordance with relevant laws and internal regulations, but encryption measures are omitted in some sections through which a user's personal information is transmitted, such as viewing and changing member information, finding a password, and changing a password
- Where passwords for accessing information systems, authentication key values, and so forth are stored in plaintext within system configuration files and source code
