## Certification criterion

Criteria and management measures for key jobs, such as handling of personal information and important information or access to key systems, shall be established, and key job holders shall be designated to the minimum extent, with the list thereof kept up to date.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are the criteria for key jobs, such as handling of personal information and important information and access to key systems, clearly defined?
2. Are executives, employees, and outsiders who perform key jobs designated as key job holders, and is the list thereof kept up to date?
3. Are those who handle personal information in the course of business designated as personal information handlers, and is the list kept up to date?
4. Are management measures, such as minimizing the designation of key job holders and personal information handlers according to business necessity, established/implemented?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special case of Information Security Management System certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [2.2.1 Designation and Management of Key Job Holders] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

The criteria for key jobs, such as handling of personal information and important information and access to key systems, shall be clearly defined.

Note: Criteria for key jobs (examples)
- Handling of important information (personal information, HR information, trade secrets, industrial secrets, financial information, etc.)
- Operation/management of important information systems (servers, databases, applications, etc.) and personal information processing systems
- Performance of information security and personal information protection management duties
- Operation of security systems, etc.

Executives, employees, and outsiders who perform key jobs shall be designated as key job holders, and the list thereof shall be kept up to date.

- Identify the status of key job holders and officially designate them as key job holders
- Manage the designated key job holders in a list
- Update the list when key job holders are newly designated, changed, or removed
- Periodically review the designation status and adequacy of key job holders to keep the list current

Those who handle personal information in the course of business shall be designated as personal information handlers, and the list shall be kept up to date.

- Manage in a list the personal information handlers who process personal information in the course of business
- The list of personal information handlers shall also include the personal information handlers of the trustee entrusted with personal information processing tasks (however, for personal information handlers of the trustee who do not have access rights to the personal information processing system, list management may be carried out by the trustee itself)
- Periodically review the designation status and adequacy of personal information handlers to keep the list current

Note: Definition of a personal information handler
- A person who processes personal information under the direction/supervision of the personal information controller, such as an executive or employee, dispatched worker, or part-time worker

Management measures, such as minimizing the designation of key job holders and personal information handlers according to business necessity, shall be established/implemented.

- Designate key job holders and personal information handlers only where truly necessary for business
- Establish an approval procedure for the application and granting of authority to key job holders and personal information handlers
- Establish/implement management and control measures for key job holders and personal information handlers (training, monitoring, etc.)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 28 (Supervision over Personal Information Handlers), Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 4 (Establishment/Implementation and Inspection of the Internal Management Plan)

## Evidence (examples)

- Criteria for key jobs
- List of key job holders
- List of personal information handlers
- Account and authority management ledger for important information systems and personal information processing systems
- Management status of key job holders (training results, security pledges, etc.)

## Nonconformity examples

- A list of key job holders (a list of personal information handlers, a list of confidential information managers, etc.) is maintained, but some executives and employees who handle large volumes of personal information or other important information (DBA, DLP administrator, etc.) are omitted from the list
- A list of key job holders and personal information handlers is maintained, but it is not kept current, for example, it includes employees who have left the company and does not include recently hired personnel
- Personal information handler authority is granted collectively at the department level, so personnel who do not actually need to handle personal information are excessively designated as personal information handlers
- The internal guideline requires that granting key job holder authority be approved by the security team and that a security pledge be signed according to the key job, but there are numerous key job holders who were registered without the security team's approval or a signed security pledge
