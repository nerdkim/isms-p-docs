## Certification criterion

The status of collected/retained personal information, including its items, volume, purpose and method of processing, and retention period, must be managed on a regular basis, and public institutions must register such status with the head of the relevant agency prescribed by law.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is the status of collected/retained personal information, including its items, volume, purpose and method of processing, and retention period, managed on a regular basis?
2. When a public institution operates or changes a personal information file, does it register the related matters with the head of the relevant agency prescribed by law?
3. Does the public institution disclose the retention status of personal information files in its privacy policy?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-3) under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the official text as relaxed/adjusted, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.2.1 Management of Personal Information Status] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

The status of collected/retained personal information, including its items, volume, purpose and method of processing, and retention period, must be managed on a regular basis.

- The personal information controller (information and communications service provider) must ascertain the items, volume, processing basis (consent, statute, etc.), purpose and method of processing, and retention period of the collected/retained personal information, and record/manage them through a personal information status table, a personal information flow table, a personal information flow diagram, etc.
- In addition, the personal information status must be checked on a regular basis and the related documents kept up to date.

When a public institution operates or changes a personal information file, it must register the related matters with the head of the relevant agency prescribed by law, and if there is any change to the registered matters, that content must also be registered.

- Upon receiving an application to register or change a personal information file, the personal information protection officer reviews the registration/change matters, judges their appropriateness, and then registers them with the Personal Information Protection Commission within 60 days.
- Affiliated agencies of central administrative agencies and local governments, and other public institutions, request the higher-level managing agency to review the relevant matters (registration/change of a personal information file) and judge their appropriateness, and then, after obtaining confirmation from the higher-level managing agency, register them with the Personal Information Protection Commission within 60 days.
- Regarding the registration and disclosure of personal information files of the National Assembly, the courts, the Constitutional Court, and the National Election Commission (including their affiliated agencies), the National Assembly Regulations, the Supreme Court Regulations, the Constitutional Court Regulations, and the National Election Commission Regulations apply.
- However, personal information files falling under Article 32(2) of the "Personal Information Protection Act" do not need to be registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission.

**Personal information files exempt from registration with the Personal Information Protection Commission (public institutions)**

1. Personal information files recording matters concerning national security, diplomatic secrets, or other significant national interests
2. Personal information files recording matters concerning criminal investigation, the institution and maintenance of prosecution, the execution of sentences and custody, correctional dispositions, protective dispositions, protective custody dispositions, and immigration control
3. Personal information files recording matters concerning the investigation of offenses under the "Punishment of Tax Evaders Act" and the investigation of offenses under the "Customs Act"
4. Personal information files, such as files operated on a one-time basis, that are recognized as having a low need for continuous management and are prescribed by Presidential Decree
   - Personal information files operated for the performance of simple tasks such as the payment of allowances for attending meetings, the delivery of materials/goods, and the settlement of money, which have a low need for continuous management
   - Personal information files processed temporarily where urgently necessary for public safety and welfare, such as public hygiene
   - Other personal information files collected solely for one-time task processing that are not stored or recorded
5. Personal information files classified as secret under other statutes
6. Personal information files collected or requested to be provided for the purpose of analyzing information related to national security
7. Personal visual information files processed through image information processing devices
8. Personal information files held by financial institutions under the "Act on Real Name Financial Transactions and Confidentiality" for the handling of financial affairs

Public institutions must disclose the retention status of personal information files in their privacy policy.

- The personal information protection officer of a public institution periodically surveys the retention/destruction status of personal information files and discloses the results in that public institution's privacy policy (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines, Article 61).
- The Personal Information Protection Commission discloses the registration status of personal information files on the internet so that anyone can easily view it (Personal Information Portal, www.privacy.go.kr).

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 32 (Registration and Disclosure of Personal Information Files)

## Evidence (examples)

- Personal information status table
- Personal information flow table/flow diagram
- Registration status of personal information files
- Personal information file management ledger
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where personal information files are managed as a list through the personal information file registration menu on a website, but the content of some personal information files related to website services is omitted from the privacy policy
- A case where two months have passed since a new personal information file was built, but the personal information file has not been registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission
- A case where the content of a personal information file registered and disclosed with the Personal Information Protection Commission (such as the items of personal information collected) differs from the actual status of the personal information file being processed
- A case where a public institution has not registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission a personal information file of its executives and employees, or a personal information file collected under the Statistics Act, even though these files do not fall under the exceptions to personal information file registration
