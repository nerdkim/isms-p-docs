## Certification criterion

Personal information must be collected and used lawfully and fairly, and where it is collected on the basis of the data subject's consent, the consent of the data subject must be obtained by lawful methods. In addition, when collecting the personal information of a child under 14 years of age, the consent of the child's legal representative must be obtained, and whether the legal representative has consented must be verified.

## Key checkpoints

1. When collecting personal information, is it collected in accordance with lawful grounds such as the data subject's consent, compliance with statutory obligations, or the conclusion/performance of a contract?
2. When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, are the consent method and timing appropriate?
3. When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, are the relevant details clearly disclosed, and are the important matters prescribed by law clearly indicated so that they are easy to recognize?
4. When obtaining consent for the collection/use/provision, etc. of the personal information of a child under 14 years of age, are the necessary matters disclosed to the legal representative and consent obtained?
5. Is only the minimum personal information necessary to obtain the legal representative's consent collected, and is a procedure and method in place to verify whether the legal representative meets the eligibility requirements?
6. When disclosing matters related to the processing of personal information, etc. to a child under 14 years of age, is it expressed in an easy-to-understand format and in clear, plain language?
7. Are records of the consent obtained from data subjects and legal representatives retained?
8. For personal information that may be processed without the data subject's consent, are its items and the legal basis for processing disclosed in the privacy policy or notified to the data subject, distinguished from personal information processed with the data subject's consent?
9. For additional use of personal information without the data subject's consent, are judgment criteria on considerations such as the relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether it infringes the data subject's interests, and safety-securing measures established/implemented, and where additional use occurs continuously, are the judgment criteria on such considerations disclosed in the privacy policy and reviewed?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the official text as relaxed/adjusted, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [3.1.1 Collection/Use of personal information], which is the same control area (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

When collecting personal information, the lawful grounds under the relevant laws, such as the data subject's consent, compliance with statutory obligations, and the conclusion/performance of a contract, must be clearly identified, and personal information must be collected lawfully accordingly.

- Clearly identify the lawful grounds for collecting personal information for each personal information collection channel, and record/manage the relevant basis so that it can be demonstrated
  - For example, where personal information is collected without the data subject's consent because there is a special provision in a statute or to comply with a statutory obligation, document the relevant basis such as the provision of the applicable statute or ordinance
- A personal information controller may collect personal information in any of the following cases, and may use it within the scope of the collection purpose

  **Cases where personal information may be collected (Article 15(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. Where the consent of the data subject is obtained
  2. Where there is a special provision in a statute or it is unavoidable to comply with a statutory obligation
  3. Where it is unavoidable for a public institution to perform its duties under statutes, etc.
  4. Where it is necessary to perform a contract concluded with the data subject or to take measures requested by the data subject in the process of concluding a contract
  5. Where it is deemed manifestly necessary for the urgent life, body, or property interests of the data subject or a third party
  6. Where it is necessary to achieve the legitimate interests of the personal information controller and such interests manifestly override the rights of the data subject. In this case, this is limited to cases where the interests are substantially related to the legitimate interests of the personal information controller and do not exceed a reasonable scope.
  7. Where it is urgently necessary for public safety and security, such as public health

When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, the consent of the data subject must be obtained by an appropriate method reflecting the characteristics of the personal information collection medium, and the information must be collected at the point in time when it is needed.

- Consent to the collection of personal information must be obtained by an appropriate method that takes into account the following matters according to the characteristics of the collection medium

  **Methods of obtaining consent to the processing of personal information (Article 17(2) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. A method of directly issuing to the data subject, or delivering by mail or fax, etc., a written document stating the content of the consent, and receiving a consent form signed or sealed by the data subject
  2. A method of informing the data subject of the content of the consent by telephone and confirming the data subject's expression of intent to consent
  3. A method of informing the data subject of the content of the consent by telephone, having the data subject confirm the consent matters through an internet address, etc., and then confirming the data subject's expression of intent to consent to those consent matters again by telephone
  4. A method of posting the content of the consent on an internet website, etc. and having the data subject indicate whether they consent
  5. A method of sending an e-mail stating the content of the consent and receiving from the data subject an e-mail stating the expression of intent to consent
  6. Any other method of informing the content of the consent and confirming the expression of intent to consent in a manner equivalent to the methods under subparagraphs 1 through 5

- Personal information must not be collected comprehensively in advance at the membership registration stage; it must be collected at the point in time when the information is needed
  - Consent to collect/use personal information must be obtained only for the personal information necessary to commence the service, and for services provided thereafter, consent must be obtained at the point of providing the relevant service
  - When registering as a website member, personal information needed only for the use of a specific service within the website is collected at the point of using that service
  - However, in the case of a repetitive service, collection/use is possible where consent is obtained by classifying it as an optional consent item at the point of first using the service

When obtaining the data subject's consent to collect personal information, the statutory matters to be disclosed must be clearly disclosed and consent obtained, and the important matters prescribed by law must be clearly indicated so that the data subject can easily recognize them.

- When obtaining consent from the data subject to collect/use personal information, the four statutory matters to be disclosed must be specifically and clearly informed and consent obtained

  **Matters to be disclosed when obtaining consent to collect/use personal information (Article 15(2) of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. The purpose of collecting/using the personal information
  2. The items of personal information to be collected
  3. The period of retention and use of the personal information
  4. The fact that the data subject has the right to refuse consent and, where there is any disadvantage resulting from the refusal of consent, the content of such disadvantage

- For the data subject's consent to be lawful, all lawful requirements must be satisfied, such as the data subject deciding whether to consent according to their free will, and the specificity and clarity of the content of the consent

  **Conditions that must be satisfied when obtaining the data subject's consent (Article 17(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. The data subject must be able to decide whether to consent according to their free will
  2. The content for which consent is sought must be specific and clear
  3. Wording that is easy to read and understand must be used
  4. The data subject must be provided with a method by which they can clearly indicate whether they consent

  * However, this provision takes effect from September 15, 2024

- Pursuant to Article 22(2) (Methods of Obtaining Consent) of the Personal Information Protection Act, when obtaining consent to the processing of personal information in writing (including electronic documents under subparagraph 1 of Article 2 of the Framework Act on Electronic Documents and Transactions), the important content must be clearly indicated so that it is easy to recognize, as follows

  **Important content that must be clearly indicated (Article 17(3) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  - The fact that, among the purposes of collecting/using personal information, the personal information may be used to contact the data subject for the promotion of goods or services or solicitation of sales, etc.
  - Among the personal information items to be processed, sensitive information, passport numbers, driver's license numbers, and alien registration numbers
  - The period of retention and use of the personal information (in the case of provision, the retention and use period of the recipient)
  - The recipient of the personal information and the recipient's purpose of using the personal information

  **Method of indicating the important content (Article 4 of the Notice on Methods of Processing Personal Information)**
  - Ensure that the content is clearly indicated through the size, color, and thickness of the font, or underlining, etc.
  - Where there are many consent matters and the important content is difficult to distinguish clearly, indicate the important content separately from the other content so that it can be easily identified

  * Considering the characteristics of the medium requiring written consent, such as printed paper or computer display screens, and the data subject's usage environment, etc., indicate it so that the data subject can easily recognize it

* For details, refer to the "Guide to Consent to Personal Information Processing (Personal Information Protection Commission)"

When obtaining consent for the collection/use/provision, etc. of the personal information of a child under 14 years of age, the necessary matters must be disclosed to the legal representative and consent obtained.

- Where there is no need to process the personal information of a child under 14 years of age, take measures not to collect the personal information of a child under 14 years of age through an appropriate age-verification procedure
- Where there is a need to process the personal information of a child under 14 years of age, take measures to obtain the legal representative's consent by preparing a separate collection consent form and a legal representative verification procedure

  **Methods of verifying whether the legal representative has consented (Article 17-2(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. A method of having the legal representative indicate whether they consent on an internet site posting the content of the consent, and having the personal information controller notify, by text message to the legal representative's mobile phone, that it has confirmed the indication of consent
  2. A method of having the legal representative indicate whether they consent on an internet site posting the content of the consent, and receiving card information such as the legal representative's credit card/debit card
  3. A method of having the legal representative indicate whether they consent on an internet site posting the content of the consent, and verifying the identity of the legal representative through mobile phone identity authentication, etc.
  4. A method of directly issuing to the legal representative, or delivering by mail or fax, a written document stating the content of the consent, and having the legal representative submit it after signing and sealing the content of the consent
  5. A method of sending an e-mail stating the content of the consent and receiving from the legal representative an e-mail stating the expression of intent to consent
  6. A method of informing the legal representative of the content of the consent by telephone and obtaining consent, or guiding a method by which the content of the consent can be confirmed, such as an internet address, and obtaining consent again through a telephone call
  7. Any other method equivalent to those under subparagraphs 1 through 6 of informing the legal representative of the content of the consent and confirming the expression of intent to consent

Only the minimum information necessary to obtain the legal representative's consent (information on name/contact details) must be collected, and a procedure and method to verify whether the legal representative meets the eligibility requirements must be prepared.

- The minimum information necessary to obtain the legal representative's consent (information on the legal representative's name/contact details) may be collected directly from the child without the legal representative's consent
  - However, when collecting the legal representative's name/contact details, the child must be informed of one's own identity and contact details, and the reason for collecting the legal representative's name and contact details (Article 13(1) of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines)
  - The legal representative's personal information collected from the child must be used only for the purpose of obtaining consent
- To obtain the legal representative's consent, whether the information provided by the child is genuinely the legal representative's information and the authenticity of the legal representative must be verified
  - Verification of whether the legal representative is a minor
  - Verification of the age difference with the child, etc.
  - * (Reference) Legal representatives of minors
    - Primarily, a person with parental authority, such as the child's parents, corresponds to the legal representative (Article 911 of the Civil Act)
    - Where a minor has no parents or the parents cannot exercise parental authority, secondarily a guardian becomes the legal representative, and the guardian follows the order of a designated guardian (Article 931 of the Civil Act) and an appointed guardian (Article 932 of the Civil Act)
- Where the legal representative refuses to consent, or the legal representative's intent to consent is not confirmed, it must be destroyed within 5 days from the date of collection (Article 13(2) of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines)

When disclosing matters related to the processing of personal information, etc. to a child under 14 years of age, it must be expressed in an easy-to-understand format and in clear, plain language.

- Convey information transparently in a child-friendly manner using language, pictures, videos, etc. that are easy for the child to understand
- Take into account the capabilities and usage behaviors of children by age group

  * For details, refer to the "Guidelines on Protection of Personal Information of Children/Adolescents (Personal Information Protection Commission)"

Records of the consent obtained from data subjects and legal representatives must be kept and retained so that the lawful basis for consent to collect/use personal information can be demonstrated.

- Matters to be recorded: date and time of consent, consent items, the consenting party (in the case of consent by a legal representative, the legal representative's information), method of consent, etc.
- Retention period: until the relevant personal information is destroyed due to membership withdrawal, etc.

For personal information that may be processed without the data subject's consent, its items and the legal basis for processing must be disclosed in the privacy policy or notified to the data subject by a written method, etc., distinguished from personal information processed with the data subject's consent.

- Cases where personal information may be collected/used without the data subject's consent: cases falling under subparagraphs 2 through 7 of Article 15(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act
- Matters to be notified to the data subject: the items of personal information that may be processed without consent and the legal basis for processing
- Method of notifying the data subject: disclose in the privacy policy, distinguished from personal information processed with consent, or notify the data subject by a written method, etc. (in writing, by e-mail, fax, telephone, text message, or an equivalent method)

For additional use of personal information without the data subject's consent, judgment criteria on considerations such as the relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether it infringes interests, and safety-securing measures must be established/implemented, and where additional use occurs continuously, this must be disclosed in the privacy policy and compliance with the criteria reviewed.

  **Considerations for additional use of personal information (Article 14-2(1) of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act)**
  1. Whether it is relevant to the original collection purpose
  2. Whether the additional use or provision of the personal information was foreseeable in light of the circumstances under which the personal information was collected or the processing practices
  3. Whether it unfairly infringes the interests of the data subject
  4. Whether measures necessary to secure safety, such as pseudonymization or encryption, have been taken

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 15 (Collection/Use of Personal Information), Article 22 (Methods of Obtaining Consent), Article 22-2 (Protection of Children's Personal Information)
- Notice on Methods of Processing Personal Information

## Evidence (examples)

- Online personal information collection forms (website membership registration screen, mobile app membership registration screen, event participation, etc.)
- Offline personal information collection forms (membership registration application, etc.)
- Records of consent to collect personal information (member database, etc.)
- Records of legal representative consent
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Where a personal information controller subject to the Personal Information Protection Act omits "the right to refuse consent and the content of the disadvantage resulting from refusal of consent" from the matters to be disclosed when obtaining consent to collect personal information
- Where, when obtaining consent to collect personal information, the items of personal information to be collected are not specifically stated but are guided comprehensively as "~ etc."
- Where, at a shopping mall website, in addition to the personal information necessary for membership registration, payment/delivery information needed later for purchasing goods is collected in advance as a mandatory item at the time of membership registration
- Where the personal information of non-members (name, e-mail, mobile phone number) is collected through Q&A or a bulletin board without going through the procedure of obtaining consent to collect personal information
- Where the personal information of a child under 14 years of age is collected without obtaining the legal representative's consent
- Where, although a service is not being provided to children under 14 years of age, an age check is not performed using the date of birth entered at the membership registration stage, and there exist member children under 14 years of age who have registered without legal representative consent
- Where the procedure for verifying the authenticity of the legal representative is inadequate, so that consent by a legal representative is possible even though the person is hard to regard as the legal representative of a child, such as a minor
- Where, after collecting the legal representative's personal information (name, mobile phone number) for the purpose of obtaining the legal representative's consent from a child under 14 years of age, the information is continuously retained without being destroyed even though the legal representative's consent has not been confirmed for a long time
- Where the personal information of a child under 14 years of age was collected on the basis of the legal representative's consent, but the relevant records are not retained, so that matters related to the legal representative's consent (legal representative's name, date and time of consent, etc.) cannot be confirmed
