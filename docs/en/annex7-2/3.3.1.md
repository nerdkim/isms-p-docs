## Certification criterion

When providing personal information to a third party, it must be based on a legal ground or the consent of the data subject, and protective measures must be established/implemented to safely protect the personal information during the provision process, including allowing the third party access to the personal information.

## Key checkpoints

1. When providing personal information to a third party, are the lawful requirements such as data subject consent and compliance with statutory obligations clearly identified and complied with?
2. When obtaining a data subject's consent for third-party provision of personal information, are the relevant matters clearly notified and the consent lawfully obtained separately from other consent matters?
3. When obtaining a data subject's consent for third-party provision of personal information, are the relevant contents clearly notified and the important matters prescribed by law clearly indicated so as to be easily recognizable?
4. When providing personal information to a third party, is it limited to the minimum personal information items appropriate for the provision purpose?
5. When providing personal information to a third party, is it provided through safe procedures and methods, and are the provision records kept and retained?
6. When allowing a third party access to personal information, is it controlled in accordance with protective procedures to safely protect the personal information?
7. For additional provision of personal information without the data subject's consent, is a judgment standard established/implemented regarding considerations such as relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether interests are infringed, and safety assurance measures, and where additional provision occurs continuously, is the judgment standard for the considerations disclosed in the privacy policy and inspected?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special provisions of information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the contents of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [3.3.1 Provision of Personal Information to Third Parties], the same control area (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

When providing personal information to a third party, the lawful requirements under relevant laws, such as data subject consent and compliance with statutory obligations, must be clearly identified, and the personal information must be lawfully provided accordingly.

- **Scope of third party**
  - All parties other than the data subject and the personal information controller that collects/holds the personal information about the data subject (other departments and organizations within the same personal information controller do not constitute a third party)

  * Third-party provision of personal information (examples)
  / Physical transfer of a storage medium containing personal information, or of printouts/booklets containing personal information
  / Transmission of personal information over a network
  / Granting a third party access rights to personal information
  / Sharing of personal information between the personal information controller and a third party
  / Any other act that results in a state of transfer or joint use of personal information

- Clearly identify the lawful requirements for personal information provision for each provision channel, and record/manage the relevant grounds so they can be proven
  - For example, where personal information is provided without the data subject's consent because there is a special provision in law or to comply with a statutory obligation, document the relevant grounds such as the provisions of the applicable statute or law

- A personal information controller may provide (including sharing) personal information to a third party in any of the following cases

  **Cases where third-party provision of personal information is permitted (Personal Information Protection Act Article 17(1))**
  1. Where the consent of the data subject is obtained
  2. Where there is a special provision in law or it is unavoidable in order to comply with a statutory obligation
  3. Where it is unavoidable for a public institution to perform its duties under its jurisdiction as prescribed by laws
  4. Where it is deemed manifestly necessary for the urgent life, bodily safety, or property interests of the data subject or a third party
  5. Where it is necessary to achieve the legitimate interests of the personal information controller and such interests manifestly override the rights of the data subject. In this case, it is limited to cases where there is a substantial connection with the legitimate interests of the personal information controller and the reasonable scope is not exceeded.
  6. Where it is urgently necessary for public safety and well-being, such as public health

  * Under items 2 through 6 above, personal information may be provided without the data subject's consent within the scope of the purpose for which the personal information was collected

Consent for third-party provision of personal information must be obtained separately from consent for collection/use, etc., and unless the third-party provision is essential for performing the core functions of the service, provision of the service must not be refused on the ground that the data subject does not consent to it.

  **Cases requiring separation of consent matters (Personal Information Protection Act Article 22(1))**
  1. Where consent is obtained under Article 15(1)1 (consent to collection/use of personal information)
  2. Where consent is obtained under Article 17(1)1 (consent to third-party provision of personal information)
  3. Where consent is obtained under Article 18(2)1 (consent to use/provision of personal information beyond the purpose)
  4. Where consent is obtained under Article 19(1) (consent to use/provision beyond the purpose by the recipient of personal information)
  5. Where consent is obtained under Article 24(1)1 (consent to processing of sensitive information)
  6. Where consent is obtained under Article 24(1)1 (consent to processing of unique identifying information)
  7. Where consent to the processing of personal information is sought in order to promote goods or services or solicit their purchase

  * Indicate separately so that the data subject can clearly know that they can choose whether to consent

When obtaining a data subject's consent for third-party provision of personal information, the relevant contents must be clearly notified and the important matters prescribed by law must be clearly indicated so as to be easily recognizable.

- When obtaining consent from a data subject for third-party provision of personal information, the five statutory notification matters must be specifically and clearly informed and consent obtained

  **Notification matters when obtaining consent for third-party provision of personal information (Personal Information Protection Act Article 17(2))**
  1. The recipient of the personal information
  2. The recipient's purpose of using the personal information
  3. The items of personal information to be provided
  4. The recipient's period of retention and use of the personal information
  5. The fact that the data subject has the right to refuse consent, and where there is any disadvantage resulting from refusal of consent, the content of such disadvantage

- For the data subject's consent to be lawful, it must satisfy all lawful requirements, such as the data subject deciding whether to consent according to their free will, and the specificity and clarity of the content of the consent

  **Conditions to be satisfied when obtaining a data subject's consent (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act Article 17(1))**
  1. The data subject can decide whether to consent according to their free will
  2. The content for which consent is sought is specific and clear
  3. Wording that can be easily read and understood is used
  4. A method by which the data subject can clearly indicate whether they consent is provided to the data subject

  * However, this provision takes effect from September 15, 2024

- Pursuant to Article 22 (Methods of Obtaining Consent) paragraph 2 of the Personal Information Protection Act, when obtaining consent to the processing of personal information in writing (including electronic documents under Article 2(1) of the Framework Act on Electronic Documents and Transactions), the important contents must be clearly indicated so as to be easily recognizable as follows

  **Important contents that must be clearly indicated (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act Article 17(3))**
  / Among the purposes of collection/use of personal information, the fact that the personal information may be used to contact the data subject for the promotion of goods or services or solicitation of their purchase, etc.
  / Among the personal information items processed, sensitive information, passport number, driver's license number, alien registration number
  / The period of retention and use of the personal information (in the case of provision, the recipient's period of retention and use)
  / The recipient of the personal information and the recipient's purpose of using the personal information

  **Method of indicating important contents (Notice on Methods of Processing Personal Information Article 4)**
  / Ensure that the content is clearly indicated through the size, color, or thickness of the text, or by underlining, etc.
  / Where there are many consent matters and it is difficult to clearly distinguish the important contents, indicate the important contents separately from the other contents so that they can be easily identified

  * For details, refer to the "Easy-to-Understand Guide to Consent for Personal Information Processing (Personal Information Protection Commission)"

When providing personal information to a third party, it must be limited to the minimum personal information items appropriate for the provision purpose.

- For third-party provision based on consent: only the minimum personal information items necessary to achieve the provision purpose notified at the time of consent must be provided
- For third-party provision based on law: only the minimum personal information items must be provided within the scope specifically stated in the statute or necessary to comply with the relevant statutory obligation

In the process of providing personal information to a third party, it must be provided through safe procedures and methods so that the personal information is not leaked/exposed, and the relevant provision records must be kept and retained.

  * Safe procedures for third-party provision (examples)
  / Clarification of the responsibility relationship regarding safety assurance between the provider and the recipient of the personal information (contracts, etc.)
  / Approval procedures related to third-party provision (in the case of provision by a person in charge)
  / Encryption of the transmission or delivery process
  / Application of safety assurance measures such as access control and access rights management
  / Retention of provision records, etc.

  * Contents to be included in third-party provision records (examples)
  / The recipient
  / Date and time of provision
  / Personal information provided: data subject identification information and personal information items
  / Purpose or ground of provision
  / Provider (person in charge): including the approver where there is an approval procedure
  / Method of provision: system linkage, email transmission, etc.
  / Other necessary information

When allowing a third party access to personal information, it must be controlled in accordance with protective procedures to safely protect the personal information.

- Safe authentication and access control measures so that only authorized persons can access
- Encryption measures to prevent eavesdropping in the transmission section
- Retention of access records so that accountability can be ensured, etc.

For additional provision of personal information without the data subject's consent, a judgment standard for considerations such as relevance to the original collection purpose, foreseeability, whether interests are infringed, and safety assurance measures must be established/implemented, and where additional provision occurs continuously, this must be disclosed in the privacy policy and compliance with the standard inspected.

  **Considerations for additional provision of personal information (Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act Article 14-2(1))**
  1. Whether it is relevant to the original collection purpose
  2. Whether there is foreseeability of the additional use or provision of the personal information in light of the circumstances under which the personal information was collected or the processing practices
  3. Whether it unfairly infringes the interests of the data subject
  4. Whether measures necessary for safety assurance, such as pseudonymization or encryption, have been taken

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 17 (Provision of Personal Information), Article 22 (Methods of Obtaining Consent)
- Notice on Methods of Processing Personal Information

## Evidence (examples)

- Online third-party provision forms (website membership registration screen, third-party provision consent screen, etc.)
- Offline third-party provision forms (membership registration application, third-party provision consent form, etc.)
- Third-party provision records
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Where the personal information controller omits some matters (right to refuse consent, items to be provided, etc.) among the matters to be notified to the data subject when obtaining consent for third-party provision of personal information
- Where, in the process of providing personal information to a third party, the consent status for third-party provision is not properly verified, so that the personal information of data subjects who did not consent is provided together
- Where, when obtaining consent for provision of personal information, consent is obtained by describing the recipient comprehensively as "~ etc." without specifying the recipient
- Where consent for third-party provision is obtained as an optional item at the membership registration stage, but the membership registration process does not proceed any further if the data subject does not consent to third-party provision
- Where excessive personal information is provided that is unrelated to the recipient's purpose of use
