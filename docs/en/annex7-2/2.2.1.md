## Certification criterion

When using external services or entrusting work to external parties, the organization shall identify the resulting information security and personal information protection requirements and specify the relevant content in a contract or agreement.

## Key checkpoints

1. When selecting external services and entrusted vendors related to the processing of critical information and personal information, is a procedure in place to consider their information security and personal information protection capabilities?
2. Are the information security and personal information protection requirements arising from the use of external services and the entrustment of work identified and specified in a contract or agreement?
3. When entrusting the development of an information system or a personal information processing system, are the information security and personal information protection requirements to be complied with during development specified in the contract?

## Detailed explanation

> ※ This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special case for information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the relaxed / adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [2.3.2 Security in external party contracts], which belongs to the same control domain (under the relaxed criterion, it applies within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

When selecting external services and entrusted vendors related to the processing of critical information and personal information, a procedure shall be in place to consider their information security and personal information protection capabilities.

- Reflect the relevant requirements in the request for proposal (RFP) and the proposal evaluation items so that a vendor with information security and personal information protection capabilities can be selected, and apply them when selecting the vendor

When entrusting the organization's information processing work to an external party or using external services, the following security requirements shall be defined and reflected in the contract.

- Compliance with laws related to information security and personal information protection, and submission of an information security and personal information protection pledge
- Periodic information security training and periodic security inspections for employees performing the entrusted work
- Measures to prevent leakage of critical information acquired in connection with performing the work
- Restriction of the external party's internet access, physical protection measures (bringing equipment and media in and out, etc.), terminal security for PCs and other devices (installing antivirus software, setting safe passwords and changing them periodically, setting screen savers, etc.), and restriction of wireless network use
- Access rights granting and revocation procedures so that excessive rights are not assigned when access to the information system is permitted
- Restriction of re-entrustment, and definition of the procedures and security requirements when re-entrustment is necessary
- Penalties for violating security requirements, liability for damages, obligation to report in the event of a security incident, etc.

  ※ Matters to be included in the document when entrusting personal information processing work (Article 26(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act and Article 28(1) of its Enforcement Decree)
  - Matters concerning the prohibition of processing personal information beyond the purpose of performing the entrusted work
  - Matters concerning technical / managerial protection measures for personal information
  - The purpose and scope of the entrusted work
  - Matters concerning the restriction of re-entrustment
  - Matters concerning measures to ensure safety, such as restricting access to personal information
  - Matters concerning supervision, such as inspecting the management status of personal information held in connection with the entrusted work
  - Matters concerning liability, such as damages, in the event the trustee violates the obligations it must comply with

When entrusting the development of an information system or a personal information processing system, the information security and personal information protection requirements to be complied with during development shall be specified in the contract.

- Compliance with legal requirements related to information security and personal information protection
- Application of development security procedures, such as compliance with secure coding standards
- Vulnerability inspection and remediation for the completed information system and personal information processing system
- Security management of the development environment, including development-related deliverables, source programs, and development data
- Obligation to maintain confidentiality of information acquired during the development process
- Matters concerning liability, such as damages, in the event of a violation, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act, Article 26 (Restrictions on Processing Personal Information Following Entrustment of Work)

## Evidence (examples)

- Entrustment contract
- Information security and personal information protection agreement (memorandum, supplementary agreement)
- Internal guidelines related to entrustment
- RFP (request for proposal) and evaluation sheet related to vendor selection

## Nonconformity examples

- The case where no entrustment contract exists for an outsourcing vendor to which IT operations, development, and personal information processing work are entrusted
- The case where the entrustment contract with an external vendor to which personal information processing work is entrusted does not include some items required by laws such as the Personal Information Protection Act (matters concerning management / supervision, etc.)
- The case where infrastructure operations and part of the personal information processing work are entrusted to an external vendor, but the contract does not identify / reflect security requirements based on the characteristics of the entrusted work and only stipulates general matters concerning confidentiality and damages
