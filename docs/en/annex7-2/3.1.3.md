## Certification criterion

Resident registration numbers may not be processed, including collection and use, except where there is a legal basis; even where processing of a resident registration number is permitted, an alternative means must be provided on internet websites and the like.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are resident registration numbers processed only where there is a clear legal basis?
2. Is the specific legal provision that serves as the basis for collecting the resident registration number identified?
3. Even where a resident registration number is processed pursuant to a legal basis, at the stage where a data subject signs up as a member through an internet website, is a method provided that allows the data subject to sign up as a member without using their resident registration number?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special provisions for certification of the information security management system. The above certification criterion / key checkpoints are the official original text as relaxed / adjusted, and the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.1.3 Restriction on Processing of Resident Registration Numbers] (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the above certification criterion / checkpoints).

Resident registration numbers may not be processed, including collection, except where there is a legal basis, as follows.

- Cases where processing of a resident registration number, including collection, is permitted (collection based on consent is not permitted)

| Personal Information Protection Act Article 24-2(1) | Network Act Article 23-2(1) |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Where an act, presidential decree, National Assembly regulation, Supreme Court regulation, Constitutional Court regulation, National Election Commission regulation, or Board of Audit and Inspection regulation specifically requires or permits the processing of resident registration numbers<br>2. Where it is deemed clearly necessary for the urgent life, bodily, or property interests of the data subject or a third party<br>3. Where processing of the resident registration number is unavoidable and the Protection Commission so provides by public notice | 1. Where designated as an identity verification agency<br>2. Where a telecommunications business operator that resells mobile communications services and the like provided by a facilities-based telecommunications business operator under Article 38(1) of the Telecommunications Business Act collects / uses a user's resident registration number in connection with the identity verification work performed by a mobile communications business operator designated as an identity verification agency under Article 23-3 |

Where a resident registration number is processed, the specific legal provision that serves as the basis for such processing must be identified so that it can be substantiated.

- "Where an act, presidential decree, National Assembly regulation, Supreme Court regulation, Constitutional Court regulation, National Election Commission regulation, or Board of Audit and Inspection regulation specifically requires or permits the processing of resident registration numbers" means that a specific provision requiring or permitting the personal information controller to process resident registration numbers exists in at least one of such acts or the like
- Since Article 24-2(1)1 of the Personal Information Protection Act limits the scope of the laws under which resident registration numbers may be processed, resident registration numbers may not be processed on the basis of enforcement rules
- Under Article 24-2(1)2 of the Personal Information Protection Act, where it is deemed clearly necessary for the urgent life, bodily, or property interests of the data subject or a third party, processing of a resident registration number is exceptionally permitted
- Unless one of the exceptional grounds set forth in each subparagraph of Article 24-2(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act applies, collecting a resident registration number, providing it to a third party, or storing / retaining it is also prohibited

* Examples of judging the lawfulness of resident registration number processing
- Enforcement rules, directives / established rules / public notices of administrative agencies at each level, and ordinances / regulations of local governments cannot serve as a basis for collecting resident registration numbers
- If a law merely stipulates an obligation such as identity verification or age verification, this does not amount to specifically stipulating a basis for processing the resident registration number
- Collecting / using not the entire resident registration number but only the last 7 digits is an act that utilizes the assignment system of the resident registration number, that is, its unique characteristics of uniqueness and identifiability, so this can be regarded as collecting / using the entire resident registration number (the same applies even when only part of the last 7 digits is processed)
- Until a job applicant finally passes and becomes an employee, there is no provision in an act or presidential decree that requires a company to process that applicant's resident registration number, so requiring the resident registration number to be entered on a resume / application form and the like is prohibited. However, after final acceptance, it is permissible for the company to collect and otherwise process the applicant's resident registration number in accordance with the relevant act or presidential decree for purposes such as enrollment in the four major insurances including employment insurance and withholding of salary
- The act of visually checking and returning an identification document bearing a resident registration number for the purpose of identity verification is not an act of collecting the resident registration number, so it does not violate the principle prohibiting resident registration number processing

Even where a resident registration number is processed pursuant to a legal basis, at the stage where a data subject signs up as a member through an internet website, a method that allows the data subject to sign up as a member without using their resident registration number (an alternative membership sign-up means to the resident registration number) must be provided.

- Examples of alternative membership sign-up means to the resident registration number: i-PIN, mobile phone, credit card, certificate, and the like

* Identification values using an alternative sign-up means (examples)
- CI (Connection Information): a one-way encrypted 88-byte string generated in the case of identity verification through an alternative means at an identity verification agency; it should be used only as external connection information, and it is desirable not to use it for internal identification
- DI (Duplication Information): a one-way encrypted 64-byte string generated in the case of identity verification through an alternative means at an identity verification agency; it is used to restrict the creation of duplicate accounts by a user within a site

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 24-2 (Restriction on Processing of Resident Registration Numbers)
- Network Act Article 23-2 (Restriction on Use of Resident Registration Numbers)

## Evidence (examples)

- Personal information collection forms (website membership sign-up screen, event participation, membership application forms, and the like)
- Online personal information collection forms (screens providing alternative sign-up means such as identity verification)
- Where a resident registration number is processed, evidence of the basis for processing the resident registration number
- Personal information processing policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Where, in connection with website sign-up, a resident registration number was collected based on the data subject's consent for the purpose of simple member management such as real-name verification
- Where a data subject's resident registration number was collected on the basis of an enforcement rule or a local government ordinance
- Where the last 6 digits of a resident registration number are collected for purposes such as identity verification when a password is lost, but there is no relevant legal basis
- Where a job applicant's resident registration number was collected without a legal basis during the recruitment process
- Where a resident registration number was collected for identity verification when inquiring about products or services at a call center
- Where, on the grounds that there is a legal basis for collecting the resident registration number, only an identity verification and membership sign-up method requiring input of the resident registration number was provided at the website membership sign-up stage, without providing an alternative sign-up means
