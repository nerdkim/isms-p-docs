## Certification criterion

The status of personal information collected/retained, such as its items, volume, processing purposes and methods, and retention period, must be managed on a regular basis, and in the case of a public institution, this must be registered with the head of the relevant agency designated by law.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is the status of personal information collected/retained, such as its items, volume, processing purposes and methods, and retention period, managed on a regular basis?
2. When a public institution operates or modifies a personal information file, are the relevant matters registered with the head of the relevant agency designated by law?
3. Does the public institution disclose the retention status of its personal information files in its privacy policy?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2) under the special cases of information security management system certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the official text as relaxed/adjusted, while the detailed explanation, related laws, evidence, and nonconformity examples below are content from the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [3.2.1 Management of Personal Information Status], which is the same control area (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion and checkpoints above).

The status of personal information collected/retained, such as its items, volume, processing purposes and methods, and retention period, must be managed on a regular basis.

- The personal information controller (information and communications service provider) must identify the items, volume, processing basis (consent, statutes, and the like), processing purposes and methods, retention period, and the like of the personal information collected/retained, and must record/manage this through a personal information status table, a personal information flow table, a personal information flow diagram, and the like
- In addition, the personal information status must be reviewed on a regular basis and the related documents must be kept up to date

When a public institution operates or modifies a personal information file, the relevant matters must be registered with the head of the relevant agency designated by law, and when there is a change to the registered matters, that content must also be registered.

- The chief privacy officer who receives an application for registration or modification of a personal information file reviews the registration/modification matters, determines their appropriateness, and then registers them with the Personal Information Protection Commission within 60 days
- Subordinate agencies of central administrative agencies and local governments, and other public institutions, request the higher supervising agency to review and determine the appropriateness of the relevant matter (personal information file registration/modification), obtain the higher supervising agency's confirmation, and then register with the Personal Information Protection Commission within 60 days
- The registration and disclosure of personal information files of the National Assembly, the courts, the Constitutional Court, and the National Election Commission (including their subordinate agencies) follow the National Assembly Regulations, the Supreme Court Regulations, the Constitutional Court Regulations, and the National Election Commission Regulations
- However, personal information files falling under Article 32 (2) of the "Personal Information Protection Act" do not need to be registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission

**Personal information files exempt from registration with the Personal Information Protection Commission (public institutions)**

1. Personal information files recording matters concerning national security, diplomatic secrets, or other significant national interests
2. Personal information files recording matters concerning criminal investigation, the institution and maintenance of prosecution, the execution of sentences and custody, correctional dispositions, protective dispositions, security surveillance dispositions, and immigration control
3. Personal information files recording matters concerning the investigation of offenses under the "Punishment of Tax Offenses Act" and the investigation of offenses under the "Customs Act"
4. Personal information files prescribed by Presidential Decree that are recognized as having a low need for continuous management, such as files operated on a one-time basis
   - Personal information files operated to perform simple tasks such as the payment of allowances for meeting attendance, the sending of materials/goods, and the settlement of money, which have a low need for continuous management
   - Personal information files processed temporarily where urgently necessary for public safety and welfare, such as public health
   - Other personal information files collected solely for one-time task processing that are not stored or recorded
5. Personal information files classified as secret under other statutes
6. Personal information files collected or requested for provision for the purpose of analyzing information related to national security
7. Personal video information files processed through image information processing devices
8. Personal information files held by financial institutions under the "Act on Real Name Financial Transactions and Confidentiality" for the handling of financial business

The public institution must disclose the retention status of its personal information files in its privacy policy.

- The chief privacy officer of the public institution periodically surveys the retention/destruction status of personal information files and discloses the results in the privacy policy of the relevant public institution (Article 61 of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines)
- The Personal Information Protection Commission discloses the personal information file registration status on the internet so that anyone can easily view it (Personal Information Portal, www.privacy.go.kr)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 32 (Registration and Disclosure of Personal Information Files)

## Evidence (examples)

- Personal information status table
- Personal information flow table/flow diagram
- Personal information file registration status
- Personal information file management ledger
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- The case where personal information files are managed as a list through the personal information file registration menu of the website, but the content of some personal information files related to website services is omitted from the privacy policy
- The case where two months have passed since a new personal information file was established, but the personal information file has not been registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission
- The case where the content of a personal information file registered with and disclosed by the Personal Information Protection Commission (such as the items of personal information collected) differs from the status of the personal information file actually being processed
- The case where a public institution has not registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission its personal information files of employees or personal information files collected under the Statistics Act, even though they do not fall under the exceptions to personal information file registration
