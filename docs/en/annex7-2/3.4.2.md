## Certification criterion

Where, even after the retention period has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, personal information is preserved without being destroyed in accordance with relevant laws, etc., it must be limited to the minimum items necessary for that purpose and must be stored/managed separately from other personal information.

## Key checkpoints

1. Where, even after the retention period of personal information has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, personal information is preserved without being destroyed in accordance with relevant laws, etc., is it managed so that only the minimum information is preserved, limited to the minimum period prescribed by relevant laws?
2. Where, even after the retention period of personal information has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, personal information is preserved without being destroyed in accordance with relevant laws, etc., is the personal information or the personal information file stored/managed separately from other personal information?
3. Is the separately stored personal information managed so that it can be processed only within the scope of the purpose prescribed by law?
4. Is access authority to the separately stored personal information limited to the minimum number of personnel?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2) under the special provisions for Information Security Management System certification. The above certification criterion / key checkpoints are the officially relaxed/adjusted text, and the Detailed explanation / Related laws / Evidence / Nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control domain, Annex 7 [3.4.2 Measures for Retention After the Processing Purpose Is Achieved] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the above certification criterion / checkpoints).

Where, even after the retention period of personal information has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, personal information is preserved without being destroyed in accordance with relevant laws, etc., it must be managed so that only the minimum information is preserved, limited to the minimum period prescribed by relevant laws.

- Limit the items of personal information to the minimum items appropriate for the retention purpose
- Set the retention period to the minimum period prescribed by relevant laws

  * Retention periods under other laws (examples)
  - Act on the Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, etc.
    (1) Records on labeling/advertising : 6 months
    (2) Records on contracts or withdrawal of subscription, etc. : 5 years
    (3) Records on payment and supply of goods, etc. : 5 years
    (4) Records on consumer complaints or dispute resolution : 3 years
  - Protection of Communications Secrets Act
    Log records of computer communications or the internet, and access location tracking data that can identify the location of information and communications devices : 3 months

Where, even after the retention period of personal information has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, personal information is preserved without being destroyed in accordance with relevant laws, etc., the personal information or the personal information file must be stored/managed separately from other personal information.

- The separated database must be configured by separating it physically or logically

Separately stored personal information must be managed so that it can be processed only within the scope of the purpose prescribed by law.

- Separately stored personal information must not be used for other purposes such as marketing

Access authority to separately stored personal information must be limited to the minimum number of personnel.

- Minimize access authority, such as by limiting access rights to the separated database to the minimum number of personnel
- Keep access records for the separated database and review them periodically, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 21 (Destruction of Personal Information)

## Evidence (examples)

- Regulations on the retention period and destruction of personal information
- Status of the separated database (table structure, etc.)
- Status of access authority to the separated database

## Nonconformity examples

- The case where withdrawn members' information is not destroyed but retained for a certain period in accordance with the Electronic Commerce Act, while only the flag value is changed and it is kept in the same table as other members' information
- The case where the legal requirements are incorrectly applied to records on consumer complaints and dispute resolution under the Electronic Commerce Act, so that they are set to be preserved for 5 years instead of 3 years
- The case where a separated database was configured, but access authority was not set separately, so that personnel who do not need access for their work can freely access the separated database
- The case where withdrawn members' information is not destroyed but records on contracts or withdrawal of subscription, payment, and supply of goods are separately preserved in accordance with the Electronic Commerce Act, yet even optional information for which there is no preservation obligation under the Electronic Commerce Act is excessively preserved
