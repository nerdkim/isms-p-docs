## Certification criterion

When collecting personal information, only the minimum personal information necessary for the processing purpose must be collected, and the provision of goods or services to the data subject must not be refused on the grounds that the data subject has not consented to matters to which the data subject may selectively consent.

## Key checkpoints

1. When personal information is collected, is only the minimum information collected within the scope necessary for the purpose?
2. When personal information is collected with the data subject's consent, is the data subject specifically informed that they may decline to consent to the collection of personal information other than the minimum information necessary?
3. Is the provision of services or goods not refused on the grounds that the data subject does not consent to the collection of personal information other than the minimum information necessary for the collection purpose?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2) under the special provisions for Information Security Management System certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the officially relaxed/adjusted text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [3.1.2 Limitation on Personal Information Collection], which is the same control domain (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

When collecting personal information, only the minimum information within the scope necessary for the purpose, such as legal basis, compliance with statutory obligations, or the conclusion/performance of a contract, must be collected.

- Even when personal information is collected with the data subject's consent, or without the data subject's consent based on a legal basis, compliance with statutory obligations, or the conclusion/performance of a contract, only the minimum personal information necessary for the purpose must be collected.
- Because the burden of proof regarding the minimum personal information rests with the personal information controller, the controller must be able to prove that the information collected as mandatory is the minimum personal information necessary for the provision of services, etc. (here, minimum personal information means information that is strictly necessary for the essential function of the relevant service).

Note: Minimum information (examples)
- The name, address, telephone number, etc. collected by a shopping business to deliver goods to a customer may be regarded as the minimum personal information necessary, but requesting personal information unrelated to delivery, such as occupation or date of birth, goes beyond the scope of minimum information.
- For a customer who entered a prize event, requesting, in addition to the personal information needed to notify them of the prize drawing, information concerning the secrecy of private life such as the entrant's gender, number of children, or cohabitation status, as well as unique identifying information, goes beyond the scope of minimum information.
- Information about a job applicant's career, major, certifications, etc. may be regarded as the minimum information necessary to assess job capability, but information about family relationships, marital status, place of origin (registered origin), etc. goes beyond the minimum information.

When personal information is collected with the data subject's consent, the data subject must be specifically informed that they may decline to consent to the collection of personal information other than the minimum information necessary.

- Inform the data subject by distinguishing which information is the minimum necessary and which is not, so that the data subject can easily identify it.
- Inform the data subject that, for information that is not the minimum necessary, they may freely refuse consent without being hindered in the use of goods or services.

The provision of services or goods must not be refused on the grounds that the data subject does not provide personal information other than the minimum information necessary for the collection purpose.

- Clearly indicate and inform the data subject that the service can be used even if the data subject refuses consent to optional items.
- Implement the system so that essential services such as membership registration can be used even if the data subject does not consent to or does not enter optional information during the membership registration process.
  - Note: For details, refer to the "Easy-to-Understand Guide to Personal Information Processing Consent (Personal Information Protection Commission)".

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 16 (Restriction on Collection of Personal Information)
- Personal Information Protection Act Article 22 (Methods of Obtaining Consent)

## Evidence (examples)

- Online personal information collection forms (website membership registration screen, event participation screen, etc.)
- Offline personal information collection forms (membership application form, etc.)
- Personal information processing policy

## Nonconformity examples

- Collecting personal information without the data subject's consent based on the conclusion and performance of a contract, while excessively collecting even personal information items that are not strictly necessary for the conclusion and performance of the contract.
- Obtaining the data subject's consent to optional items without specifically informing them that they may decline to consent to the collection of the relevant personal information.
- The membership registration form was set up to obtain separate consent by distinguishing between mandatory and optional information, but the data subject was not specifically informed so that they could recognize that membership registration is possible even without consenting to optional information (e.g., the personal information entry form does not indicate whether each personal information item is mandatory or optional).
- On the website membership registration screen, if the data subject does not consent to optional items or does not enter optional information, the process does not proceed to the next step or membership registration is blocked.
- Collecting excessive personal information, such as family details not directly related to the intended job duties, when concluding an employment contract.
