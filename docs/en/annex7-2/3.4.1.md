## Certification criterion

Internal policies related to the retention period and destruction of personal information shall be established, and when the point of destruction has arrived, such as expiry of the retention period or achievement of the processing purpose, the personal information shall be destroyed without delay by a method that ensures the safety and completeness of destruction.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are internal policies related to the retention period and destruction of personal information established?
2. When the processing purpose of personal information has been achieved or the retention period has expired, is the relevant personal information destroyed without delay?
3. When destroying personal information, is it destroyed by a safe method so that it cannot be recovered or reproduced?
4. Are records of the destruction of personal information kept and managed?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2) under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the official, relaxed / adjusted text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are drawn from the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [3.4.1 Destruction of Personal Information] (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

Internal policies related to the retention period and destruction of personal information shall be established, including the following matters.

- For each collected item, each collection purpose, and each collection channel: the storage location (database, backup data, etc.), destruction method, point of destruction, legal basis, and so on
- Public institutions shall establish and implement a personal information destruction plan that reflects the retention period, processing purpose, etc. of the personal information files, and may include the personal information destruction plan in the internal management plan (Article 55(2) of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines)

When the processing purpose of personal information has been achieved or the retention period has expired, the relevant personal information shall be destroyed without delay.

- When the personal information has become unnecessary, such as achievement of the processing purpose, discontinuation of the relevant service, termination of the business, or expiry of the retention period under the law, the personal information shall be destroyed within 5 days therefrom unless there is a justifiable reason

  Note: Cases where the purpose of collecting and using personal information has been achieved (examples)
  / When the data subject withdraws from membership of a website
  / When the data subject cancels high-speed internet service
  / When the data subject requests withdrawal from mileage membership
  / When an event for which personal information is collected has ended
  / When the telemarketing (TM) work of a third-party company has ended after information was provided to that company for telemarketing, etc.

When destroying personal information, it shall be destroyed by a safe method so that it cannot be recovered or reproduced. Here, an irreversible method means a method of taking measures so that, at the current level of technology and at a cost reasonable by social norms, the destroyed personal information cannot be restored.

- Complete destruction (incineration / shredding, etc.)
- Deletion using dedicated degaussing equipment (equipment that erases the data on a storage device using a magnetic field)
- Performing initialization or overwriting so that the data cannot be restored

  Note: Destruction methods (examples)
  / Complete destruction: destroying paper documents such as membership application forms containing personal information, or hard disks or magnetic tapes, by a shredder, or by dissolution, or by burning at an incineration site or incinerator, etc.
  / When using dedicated degaussing equipment: erasing personal information stored on hard disks or magnetic tapes using a degausser, etc.
  / When performing initialization or overwriting so that the data cannot be restored: performing a full format on the hard disk where personal information is stored (3 or more times recommended), overwriting the data area with random values, 0, 1, etc. (3 or more times recommended), or encrypting and storing the relevant drive with a safe algorithm and key length and then deleting it, completely destroying the key used for encryption and overwriting with random values, etc.

- Where only part of the personal information is to be destroyed and it is difficult to destroy it by the above methods, the following measures shall be carried out
  1. In the case of an electronic file: after deleting the personal information, manage and supervise so that it cannot be recovered or reproduced
  2. In the case of records other than electronic files, printed matter, written documents, and other recording media: delete the relevant part by masking, punching holes, etc.
- Where it is remarkably difficult to destroy by the above methods due to technical characteristics, take measures to make restoration impossible by processing the information into information falling under Article 58-2 of the Act (anonymization)

Records of the destruction of personal information shall be kept and managed.

- The implementation of destruction of personal information and the confirmation of destruction results shall be carried out under the responsibility of the personal information protection officer, and matters concerning destruction shall be recorded / managed
- Records of the destruction of personal information shall be recorded in a destruction management ledger, or photographs, etc. containing the details of destruction shall be kept as records
- When a public institution destroys a personal information file, it shall confirm the destruction result and prepare a personal information file destruction management ledger (Article 55 of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act, Article 21 (Destruction of Personal Information)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information, Article 13 (Destruction of Personal Information)

## Evidence (examples)

- Regulations related to the retention period and destruction of personal information
- Personal information destruction results (member database, etc.)
- Personal information destruction management ledger

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, when the purpose has been achieved, such as membership withdrawal, or the retention period has expired, the relevant personal information was destroyed in the member database, but personal information that had been copied and stored in linked personal information processing systems such as CRM / DW was not destroyed
- A case where, for personal information collected during an event held for a specific period, no destruction criteria were established or destruction was not carried out even after the event ended
- A case where personal information related to civil complaint handling collected at a call center (consultation history, recordings, etc.) is retained for 3 years on the basis of the Act on Electronic Commerce, but is kept without being destroyed even after 3 years have passed
- A case where, because complete destruction of personal information whose purpose has been achieved is difficult due to technical characteristics such as blockchain, anonymization was performed instead of complete destruction, but the anonymization was not carried out properly, making re-identification or other restoration of some personal information possible
