# 2.1.1 Security Pledge  _( Annex 7-2 )_

## Certification criterion

Employees, temporary staff, external parties, and others who handle information assets or are granted access rights must sign an information protection pledge appropriate to the nature of their duties, so that they clearly recognize matters to comply with, such as internal policies, relevant laws, and confidentiality obligations.

## Key checkpoints

1. When recruiting new personnel, is an information protection and personal information protection pledge that specifies information protection and personal information protection responsibilities obtained?
2. When granting access rights to information assets to external parties such as temporary staff and outsourced service personnel, is a pledge that specifies responsibilities for information protection and personal information protection, confidentiality obligations, and the like obtained?
3. When employees resign, is a separate pledge related to confidentiality obtained?
4. Are the information protection, personal information protection, and confidentiality pledges kept safely and managed so that they can be easily retrieved when needed?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2) under the special cases of information security management system certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the official text as relaxed/adjusted, while the detailed explanation, related laws, evidence, and nonconformity examples below are content from the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [2.2.3 Security Pledge], which is the same control area (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion and checkpoints above).

When recruiting new personnel, an information protection and personal information protection pledge that specifies information protection and personal information protection responsibilities must be obtained.

- When new personnel join, they sign a pledge specifying the necessity of and responsibility for information protection and personal information protection, compliance with internal policies and relevant laws, and confidentiality obligations
- When significant changes such as changes in employment conditions occur, take measures such as re-signing the pledge

When granting access rights to information assets (including personal information), information systems, and the like to external parties such as temporary staff and outsourced service personnel, they must be required to sign a pledge specifying responsibilities for information protection and personal information protection, confidentiality obligations, and the like.

- Include necessary content such as responsibility for information protection and personal information protection, confidentiality obligations, the obligation to comply with internal regulations and relevant laws, and liability for damages in the event of an incident/accident caused by failure to comply with the relevant obligations

When employees resign, a separate pledge related to confidentiality must be obtained.

- Obtain a confidentiality pledge (included within the resignation process) so that resigning persons clearly recognize that they bear legal responsibility in the event of an information leak

Information protection, personal information protection, and confidentiality pledges must be preserved safely and managed so that they can be easily retrieved when needed.

- Store/manage them safely in a lockable cabinet or a document storage room with access control applied, so that they can be used as evidence of legal responsibility in the event of a legal dispute

## Related laws

_No individual legal provision directly corresponding to this item is specified._

## Evidence (examples)

- Information protection and personal information protection pledge (employees, external personnel)
- Confidentiality pledge (resigning persons)

## Nonconformity examples

- The case where the recruitment procedure stipulates that new hires must sign a security pledge, but the security pledge of some recently hired employees was omitted
- The case where security pledges are obtained from employees, but security pledges are not obtained from outsourced personnel who can directly access the information processing system
- The case where management is inadequate, such as a file binder collecting submitted information protection and personal information protection pledges being left on an office desk where unauthorized persons can access it
- The case where only a security pledge is obtained from personal information handlers, but the security pledge contains only content on confidentiality and does not include responsibilities and content regarding personal information protection
