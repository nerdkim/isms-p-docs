## Certification criterion

User accounts must be assigned identifiers so that each user can be uniquely distinguished, and the use of guessable identifiers must be restricted. Where the same identifier is shared and used, the reason and justification must be reviewed, and supplementary measures such as obtaining the approval of the responsible manager and ensuring accountability (traceability) must be established and implemented.

## Key checkpoints

1. In the information system and the personal information processing system, are identifiers that can uniquely distinguish each user and each personal information handler assigned, and is the use of guessable identifiers restricted?
2. Where the same identifier is shared and used for unavoidable reasons, is the reason and justification reviewed, are supplementary measures prepared, and is the approval of the responsible manager obtained?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special provision for information security management system certification. The certification criterion and key checkpoints above are the official, relaxed/adjusted text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [2.5.2 User identification], which belongs to the same control area (under the relaxed criteria, it applies within the scope of the certification criterion and checkpoints above).

When registering users for the information system and the personal information processing system, an identifier that can uniquely distinguish each user and each personal information handler must be assigned, and the use of guessable identifiers must be restricted.

- Ensure accountability (traceability) for users by adopting the principle of issuing one account per person
- Restrict account sharing and the use of shared (common) accounts
- Restrict general users' access to the operating accounts used by the system
- After a system is installed, remove the default accounts and test accounts of the manufacturer or vendor, or change them to accounts that are difficult to guess (including changing default passwords)
- Restrict the use of easily guessable identifiers (root, admin, administrator, etc.) for administrator and special-privilege accounts

Where the same identifier is unavoidably shared and used for business reasons, the reason and justification must be reviewed and the approval of the responsible manager must be obtained.

- Even where an administrator account is shared because the primary/backup roles are separated in the division of duties, assign a separate user account, log in with the user account, and then switch to the administrator account
- Where an account is temporarily shared for maintenance work, etc., change the password of that account immediately after the work is completed
- Where the use of a common account is unavoidably necessary for business reasons, review the reason and justification, obtain the approval of the responsible manager, and apply additional control measures to guarantee accountability (traceability)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act, Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information, Article 5 (Management of Access Rights)

## Evidence (examples)

- Login screens of the information system and the personal information processing system
- Account lists of administrators, users, and personal information handlers of the information system and the personal information processing system
- Approval records for exception handling

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, upon reviewing the account status of information systems (servers, network devices, intrusion prevention systems, DBMS, etc.), the default administrator account provided by the manufacturer is being used without being changed even though it can technically be changed
- A case where a developer is using a personal information processing system account in common, but is doing so without any justification review or approval of the responsible manager
- A case where the operating account of an information system maintained by external staff is being used like a personal account without a separate approval procedure
