## Certification criterion

To prevent unauthorized acts and abuse/misuse of privileges within protected areas, work procedures shall be established/implemented, and the history of items brought in and out shall be reviewed periodically.

## Key checkpoints

1. When work within a protected area is necessary due to information system introduction, maintenance, etc., are formal work application and execution procedures established/implemented?
2. Are work records reviewed periodically to verify whether work within protected areas has been properly performed in accordance with the control procedures?
3. When information systems, mobile devices, storage media, etc. are brought into or out of a protected area, are control procedures established/implemented to prevent security incidents such as information leakage and malware infection?
4. Are records maintained/managed in accordance with the in/out control procedures, and is the in/out history inspected periodically to verify compliance with the procedures?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special case of Information Security Management System certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official text, and the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for the same control area, Annex 7 [2.4.6 Control of Devices Brought In and Out] (under the relaxed criteria, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

When information systems, mobile devices, storage media, etc. are brought into or out of a protected area, control procedures shall be established/implemented to prevent security incidents such as information leakage and malware infection.

- Targets of in/out control : information systems (servers, network equipment, etc.), mobile devices (laptops, smart pads, smartphones, etc.), storage media (HDD, SSD, USB memory, external hard disks, CD/DVD, tapes, etc.), etc.
- In/out control procedures : prior approval by the person responsible for protected-area access control, recording in the in/out management ledger, performing security checks on the devices brought in/out (whether antivirus is installed, whether security updates are applied, whether malware is present, whether a security sticker is attached, whether important information is leaked, etc.), periodic review of in/out records, etc.
- Exception use procedure : exception application/approval, recording in the in/out management ledger, etc.

* In/out management ledger record items (examples)
- Date/time and location of bringing in/out
- User information
- Model, device identification information (serial number, etc.)
- Reason for bringing in/out
- Security check results
- Manager confirmation signature, etc.

Records shall be maintained/managed in accordance with the in/out control procedures, and the in/out history shall be inspected periodically to verify compliance with the procedures.

- Maintaining records of the in/out history within protected areas (in/out management ledger, in/out control system logs, etc.)

Inspect the in/out history periodically to review whether items brought in/out of protected areas have been handled properly in accordance with the control procedures.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 10 (Physical Safety Measures)

## Evidence (examples)

- Application for bringing devices in/out of protected areas
- In/out management ledger
- Results of in/out history review

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where control procedures for bringing mobile computing devices in/out are established, but there is no control over bringing mobile computing devices into a controlled area, so internal/external persons permitted to enter use mobile computing devices without restriction
- A case where, under internal guidelines, in/out of computing equipment must be recorded in a work plan and signed by the responsible manager, but the responsible manager's signature is missing from many of the in/out records in the work plan
