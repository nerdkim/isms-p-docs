# 2.9.4 Business Device Security  _( Annex 7-2 )_

## Certification criterion

When devices such as PCs and mobile devices are connected to the network for business purposes, access control measures such as device authentication and approval, access scope, and device security settings must be established and periodically reviewed.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are security control policies such as device authentication, approval, access scope setting, and device security settings established and implemented for devices used in business, such as PCs, laptops, virtual PCs, and tablets?
2. To prevent personal information and important information from being leaked through business devices, are policies such as prohibiting the use of file-sharing programs, restricting share settings, and controlling the use of wireless networks established and implemented?
3. Are security measures applied to prevent the leakage/exposure of personal information and important information due to loss or theft of business mobile devices?
4. Is the adequacy of access control measures for business devices periodically reviewed?

## Detailed explanation

> ※ This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special provisions for information security management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [2.10.6 Business Device Security], which is the same control area (under the relaxed criteria, they are applied within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

Security control policies such as device authentication, approval, access scope setting, and device security settings must be established and implemented for devices used in business, such as PCs, laptops, virtual PCs, and tablets.

- Criteria for permitting business devices
- Scope of business use through business devices
- Approval procedures and methods when using business devices
- Authentication methods when connecting to the business network : device authentication, MAC authentication, etc.
- Security setting policies for the use of business devices, such as vaccine (anti-virus) installation and security program installation
- Security setting policies and misuse/abuse monitoring measures for the use of business devices, etc.

※ Security management and monitoring measures for the use of business devices (examples)
- User security setting policies for business devices (vaccine installation, security patches, caution when used in public places, data initialization upon loss, etc.)
- Policies, education, assignment of responsibility, and punishment criteria to prevent the leakage of personal information and internal data
- Monitoring measures to identify misuse/abuse of business devices
- Measures to inspect the safety of software installed on business devices
- Measures to prevent malicious code on business devices
- Access control measures for business devices to prevent the leakage/exposure of personal information via the internet, open wireless networks, etc.

To prevent personal information and important information from being leaked through business devices, policies such as prohibiting the use of file-sharing programs, restricting share settings, and controlling the use of wireless networks must be established and implemented.

- When share settings are unavoidably applied, set an access authorization password on the business device, and remove the share settings after use is completed
- When file transfer is the main purpose, grant only read permission, and set write permission individually only when the other party needs to write
- Prevent the intentional/negligent leakage/exposure of personal information and important information through P2P programs, commercial webmail, web hard drives, messengers, SNS services, etc.
- Use of wireless networks to which security protocols such as WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 2) are applied, etc.

To prevent the leakage/exposure of personal information and important information due to loss or theft of business mobile devices, security measures such as setting passwords must be applied.

※ Loss/theft measures for business mobile devices (examples)
- Set screen lock using a password, pattern, PIN, fingerprint, iris, etc.
- Encrypt applications, data, etc. using device encryption functions
- Remote lock and remote data deletion using functions provided by the mobile device manufacturer or telecommunications carrier (kill switch service, etc.)
- For mobile devices that process important personal information, install mobile device management programs such as MDM (Mobile Device Management) for remote lock, remote data deletion, access control, etc.

The adequacy of access control measures for business devices must be periodically reviewed.

- History of business device application/approval, registration/release, and device authentication
- Status of business device security settings, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Secure the Safety of Personal Information Article 6 (Access Control)

## Evidence (examples)

- Business device security control guidelines and procedures
- Business device registration status
- Business device security settings
- Business device authentication and approval history
- Business device security inspection status

## Nonconformity examples

- The case where mobile devices such as laptops and tablet PCs are used for business purposes, but policies on the permission criteria, scope of use, approval procedures, and authentication methods for business mobile devices have not been established
- The case where the mobile device security management guidelines in principle prohibit the business use of mobile devices and specify that, when necessary, only mobile devices permitted for a limited period through an approval procedure may be used, but permitted mobile devices are not identified/managed and access to internal information systems is possible even from unapproved mobile devices
- The case where protective measures against theft/loss, such as setting passwords, are not applied to mobile devices used for personal information processing tasks
- The case where internal regulations prohibit the use of shared folders on business devices, but periodic reviews are not conducted, so shared folders are excessively set and used on many business devices
