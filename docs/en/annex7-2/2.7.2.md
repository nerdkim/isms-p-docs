# 2.7.2 Separation of Test and Operational Environments  _( Annex 7-2 )_

## Certification criterion

Development and test systems must, in principle, be separated from the operational system in order to reduce the risk of unauthorized access to and changes of the operational system.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are the development and test systems of the information system separated from the operational system?
2. Where separation of the development and operational environments is difficult for unavoidable reasons, are security measures such as mutual review, monitoring by superiors, change approval, and securing accountability in place?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2) under the special case of information security management system certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official text, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [2.8.3 Separation of test and operational environments], which belongs to the same control domain (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

The development and test systems of the information system must be separated from the operational system.

- In principle, configure the development and test systems separately from the operational system
- Establish/implement access control measures between the development and test systems and the operational system so that developers cannot unnecessarily access the operational system

Where separation of the development and operational environments is difficult for unavoidable reasons, security measures such as mutual review, monitoring by superiors, change approval, and securing accountability must be prepared.

- Where separation of the development and operational environments is unavoidably difficult due to reasons such as a very small organization size, lack of human resources, or system characteristics, apply appropriate compensating controls so as to reduce the resulting security risks
  - Mutual review among duty holders
  - Change approval
  - Monitoring and audit by superiors
  - Backup and recovery measures, securing accountability, etc.

## Related laws

_No individual legal provision directly corresponding to this item is specified._

## Evidence (examples)

- Network configuration diagram (including test environment configuration)
- Status of access control applied between the operational environment and the development/test environment

## Nonconformity examples

- Where source code changes are performed directly in the operational environment without a valid reason or approval, without configuring a separate development environment
- Where the development system and operational system are unavoidably operated without separation, but records of mutual review, monitoring, etc. are missing
- Where a development system is configured separately, but access from the development environment to the operational environment is not controlled, so that developers can unnecessarily access the operational system via the development system
