## Certification criterion

Information protection policies and implementation documents must be established/prepared, and in doing so the organization's information protection principles and direction must be presented clearly. In addition, the policies and implementation documents must be approved by management and delivered to executives, employees, and relevant parties in an easily understandable form.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is a top-level information protection policy that includes the basis for all information protection activities carried out by the organization being established?
2. Are guidelines, procedures, manuals, etc. that specify the detailed methods, procedures, cycles, etc. necessary for implementing the information protection policy being established?
3. When enacting/amending information protection policies/implementation documents, is approval being obtained from the top management or a person delegated with authority by the top management?
4. Is the latest version of the information protection policies/implementation documents being provided to relevant executives and employees in an easily accessible form?

## Detailed explanation

> Note: This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the special provisions for information protection management system certification. The certification criterion/key checkpoints above are the relaxed/adjusted official original text, while the detailed explanation/related laws/evidence/nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [1.1.5 Policy Establishment], which is the same control area (under the relaxed criterion, they apply within the scope of the certification criterion/checkpoints above).

The top-level information protection and personal information protection policy that includes the basis for all information protection and personal information protection activities carried out by the organization must be established including the following content.

- The will and direction of management, including the top management, regarding the organization's information protection and personal information protection
- The roles/responsibilities and targets/scope for the organization's information protection and personal information protection
- The basis for the administrative/technical/physical information protection and personal information protection activities carried out by the organization

To specifically implement the information protection and personal information protection matters specified in the information protection and personal information protection policy, lower-level implementation documents such as guidelines, procedures, manuals, and guides that specify the necessary detailed methods, procedures, cycles, performing entities, etc. must be established to fit the characteristics of the organization.

- The lower-level implementation documents must be able to specifically present the basis for all information protection and personal information protection activities carried out by the organization, and must be established to fit the organization's characteristics from various perspectives such as the protection-target perspective or the performing-entity perspective
  - Note: Lower-level implementation documents (examples)

  | Protection-target perspective | Performing-entity perspective |
  | --- | --- |
  | Server security guideline | Executive/employee security guideline |
  | Network security guideline | Developer security guideline |
  | Database security guideline | Operator security guideline, etc. |
  | Application security guideline | |
  | Web service security guideline | |
  | Cloud security guideline | |

- Policies and implementation documents (guidelines, procedures, etc.) reflect the legal requirements related to personal information protection (laws, enforcement decrees, enforcement rules, subordinate notices, guides, etc.) related to the services, businesses, etc. provided by the organization
- When processing personal information, an internal management plan under the Personal Information Protection Act must be established including all matters required by the related laws
  - Matters that must be included in the internal management plan under the Personal Information Protection Act
    1. Matters concerning the composition and operation of the personal information protection organization
    2. Matters concerning the qualification requirements and designation of the personal information protection officer
    3. Matters concerning the roles and responsibilities of the personal information protection officer and the personal information handlers
    4. Matters concerning the management/supervision and education of personal information handlers
    5. Matters concerning the management of access rights
    6. Matters concerning access control
    7. Matters concerning encryption measures for personal information
    8. Matters concerning the retention and inspection of access records
    9. Matters concerning the prevention of malicious programs, etc.
    10. Matters concerning vulnerability inspection to prevent the leakage and theft of personal information, etc.
    11. Matters concerning physical safety measures
    12. Matters concerning the establishment/implementation of a personal information leakage incident response plan
    13. Matters concerning risk analysis and management
    14. Matters concerning the management and supervision of trustees when personal information processing tasks are entrusted
    15. Matters concerning the establishment, modification, and approval of the personal information internal management plan
    16. Other matters necessary for the protection of personal information
    - Note: However, for small business owners/individuals/organizations that process personal information concerning fewer than 10,000 data subjects, this may be omitted

When enacting/amending information protection and personal information protection policies/implementation documents, approval must be obtained from the top management or a person delegated with authority by the top management.

- When enacting/amending the policy document and implementation documents, sufficiently consult/review the relevant content with stakeholders
- Consider the impact on the organization's tasks and services and legal compliance, etc. caused by changes to the policy document and implementation documents
- Keep records of reviewed matters such as meeting minutes and reflect the relevant matters in the policies/guidelines, etc.
- Report the policy document and implementation documents whose review has been completed to management and obtain approval

When enacting/amending information protection and personal information protection policies/implementation documents, the latest version must be provided to relevant executives and employees in an easily understandable form.

- Provide it in a form that executives, employees, and external parties can easily refer to (electronic bulletin board, booklet, education materials, manuals, etc.)
- Whenever enactment/amendment matters arise, the policy document and implementation documents must be announced immediately and the latest version maintained

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Secure Safety of Personal Information Article 4 (Establishment/Implementation and Inspection of the Internal Management Plan)

## Evidence (examples)

- Information protection and personal information protection policies/guidelines/procedures (including enactment/amendment history)
- Stakeholder review meeting minutes at the time of enacting/amending information protection and personal information protection policies/guidelines/procedures
- Personal information internal management plan
- Notification records of the enactment/amendment of information protection and personal information protection policies/guidelines (groupware, in-house bulletin board, etc.)
- Information protection and personal information protection committee meeting minutes

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, according to internal regulations, the enactment/amendment of the information protection and personal information protection policy document must go through a resolution of the information protection and personal information protection committee, but in a recent amendment of the policy document the matter was not brought to the committee as an agenda item and was amended solely on the basis of the approval of the Chief Information Security Officer and the Chief Privacy Officer
- A case where the information protection and personal information protection policies and guidelines were recently amended, but the matter was not shared/delivered to the relevant departments and executives/employees, so some departments are performing tasks based on an old version of the guidelines
- A case where the information protection and personal information protection policies and guidelines are managed only by the security department, and are not provided in a way such as a bulletin board or documents so that executives and employees can view them
