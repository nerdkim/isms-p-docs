## Certification criterion

In order to maintain the availability and data integrity of information systems, procedures for backup scope, cycle, method, storage location, retention period, and off-site dispersal must be established and implemented. In addition, management must ensure timely recovery in the event of an incident.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are backup and recovery procedures that include backup scope, cycle, method, and steps established and implemented?
2. Are recovery tests conducted regularly to verify the completeness and accuracy of the backed-up information and the adequacy of the recovery procedures?
3. For backup media on which important information is stored, are the backup media dispersed to a physically separate location so that disasters/catastrophes can be dealt with?

## Detailed explanation

> * This item is a **relaxed certification criterion (Annex 7-2)** under the exception to Information Security Management System certification. The certification criterion / key checkpoints above are the official text as relaxed/adjusted, while the detailed explanation / related laws / evidence / nonconformity examples below are the content of the "ISMS-P Certification Criteria Guide" for Annex 7 [2.9.3 Backup and recovery management], which belongs to the same control area (under the relaxed criteria, they are applied within the scope of the certification criterion / checkpoints above).

Backup and recovery procedures that include backup scope, cycle, method, and steps must be established and implemented so that timely recovery is possible when an information system is damaged by a disaster/catastrophe, failure, or security breach.

- Establishment of criteria for selecting backup targets
- Designation of backup personnel and responsible officers
- Definition of backup cycle and retention period for each backup target
- Backup method and procedure: use of a backup system, manual method, etc.
- Backup media management (e.g.: labeling, storage location, access control, etc.)
- Backup recovery procedure: for key information systems, from an IT disaster recovery perspective, periodic recovery tests are needed to check the completeness, accuracy, etc. of backup information
- Management of a backup management ledger, etc.

* Main backup targets (examples) (selected in consideration of the importance of the target information and information systems)
- Important information (personal information, confidential information, etc.)
- Important databases
- Various logs (information system audit logs, event logs, security system event logs, etc.)
- Configuration files, etc.

Recovery tests must be conducted regularly to verify the completeness and accuracy of the backed-up information and the adequacy of the recovery procedures.

- Recovery test plan (recovery test cycle and timing, person in charge, method, etc.)
- Establishment of a recovery test scenario
- Conducting the recovery test and reporting the results
- Establishment and implementation of an improvement plan when problems are found in the recovery test results

For backup media on which important information is stored, the backup media must be dispersed to a physically separate location so that disasters/catastrophes can be dealt with.

- Backup media on which important information is stored are dispersed and kept at a location physically distant from where the operating information system or backup system is located, and the dispersal history is managed through a management ledger
  - Dispersal date (removal, receipt, etc.)
  - Dispersed backup media and backup information content
- Periodic checks on whether dispersal is being carried out appropriately
- Preparation of the following security measures for the dispersal location
  - Measures against natural disasters such as fire and flood (e.g.: fireproof safe, flame-retardant treatment, etc.)
  - Access control for the dispersal location and media, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 11 (Safety measures for disaster/catastrophe preparedness)

## Evidence (examples)

- Backup and recovery procedures
- Recovery test results
- Status of dispersed backups

## Nonconformity examples

- When backup and recovery procedures that include backup scope, cycle, method, and steps have not been established
- When a backup policy has been established but backup target information that must be retained for a long period (6 months, 3 years, 5 years, etc.) under legal requirements is not being retained in accordance with the backup policy
- When backup is not being carried out for some systems (security system policies and logs, etc.) that are specified in higher-level guidelines or internal guidelines to be separately backed up and managed
- When higher-level guidelines or internal guidelines require periodic recovery tests of backup media, but recovery tests have not been conducted for a long period
