# 2.10.6 Security of Business Terminals

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Domain | 2. Protective Measure Requirements |
| Section | 2.10 System and Service Security Management |
| Item | 2.10.6 Security of Business Terminals |
| Applicable certification | ISMS `●` / ISMS-P `●` |
| Korean source | 2.10.6 업무용 단말기기 보안 |

## Certification criterion

When terminals such as PCs and mobile devices are connected to the network for business purposes, access control measures including device authentication and approval, access scope, and device security settings must be established and reviewed periodically.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are security control policies such as device authentication, approval, access scope configuration, and device security settings established and implemented for terminals used for business, including PCs, laptops, virtual PCs, and tablets?
2. Are policies such as prohibition of file-sharing programs, restriction of sharing settings, and control of wireless network use established and implemented to prevent the leakage of personal information and important information through business terminals?
3. Are security measures applied to prevent the leakage/exposure of personal information and important information due to loss or theft of business mobile devices?
4. Is the adequacy of access control measures for business terminals reviewed periodically?

## Detailed explanation

Security control policies such as device authentication, approval, access scope configuration, and device security settings must be established and implemented for terminals used for business, including PCs, laptops, virtual PCs, and tablets.

- Criteria for permitting business terminals
- Scope of business use through business terminals
- Approval procedures and methods for using business terminals
- Authentication methods when connecting to the business network: device authentication, MAC authentication, etc.
- Security setting policy for business terminal use, such as installation of antivirus and security programs
- Security setting policy for business terminal use and measures for monitoring misuse/abuse, etc.

* Security management and monitoring measures for business terminal use (examples)
- User security setting policy for business terminals (antivirus installation, security patches, caution when using in public places, data wiping upon loss, etc.)
- Policy, training, assignment of responsibility, and disciplinary standards to prevent leakage of personal information and internal data
- Monitoring measures to identify whether business devices are misused/abused
- Measures to review the safety of software installed on business devices
- Measures to prevent malicious code on business devices
- Access control measures for business devices to prevent leakage/exposure of personal information through the internet, open wireless networks, etc.

To prevent the leakage of personal information and important information through business terminals, policies such as prohibition of file-sharing programs, restriction of sharing settings, and control of wireless network use must be established and implemented.

- When sharing settings are unavoidable, set an access-permission password on the business terminal, and remove the sharing setting after use is complete
- When file transfer is the main purpose, grant only read permission, and set write permission individually only when the other party needs to write
- Prevent intentional/careless leakage/exposure of personal information and important information through P2P programs, commercial webmail, web-hard, messengers, SNS services, etc.
- Use of wireless networks to which security protocols such as WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 2) are applied, etc.

To prevent the leakage/exposure of personal information and important information due to loss or theft of business mobile devices, security measures such as password setting must be applied.

* Measures against loss/theft of business mobile devices (examples)
- Set screen lock using passwords, patterns, PINs, fingerprints, iris recognition, etc.
- Encrypt applications, data, etc. using device encryption functions, etc.
- Remote lock and remote data wipe using functions provided by the mobile device manufacturer or the mobile carrier (kill switch service, etc.)
- For mobile devices that process important personal information, install a mobile device management program such as MDM (Mobile Device Management) to enable remote lock, remote data wipe, access control, etc.

The adequacy of access control measures for business terminals must be reviewed periodically.

- History of application/approval, registration/deregistration, and device authentication of business terminals
- Status of security settings of business terminals, etc.

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 29 (Duty of Safety Measures)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 6 (Access Control)

## Evidence (examples)

- Guidelines and procedures for security control of business terminals
- Registration status of business terminals
- Security settings of business terminals
- History of device authentication and approval of business terminals
- Security review status of business terminals

## Nonconformity examples

- Mobile devices such as laptops and tablet PCs are used for business purposes, but no policy has been established regarding the permission criteria, scope of use, approval procedures, authentication methods, etc. for business mobile devices
- The mobile device security management guidelines stipulate that the use of mobile devices for business is prohibited in principle, and that when necessary only approved mobile devices may be used for a limited period through an approval procedure, but the approved mobile devices are not identified/managed and access to internal information systems is possible even from unapproved mobile devices
- Protective measures against theft/loss, such as password setting, are not applied to mobile devices used for personal information processing tasks
- Internal regulations prohibit the use of shared folders on business terminals, but periodic reviews are not conducted, so shared folders are excessively configured and used on many business terminals
