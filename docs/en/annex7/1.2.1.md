# 1.2.1 Information Asset Identification

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Domain | 1. Establishment and operation of the management system |
| Section | 1.2 Risk management |
| Item | 1.2.1 Information asset identification |
| Applicable certification | ISMS `●` / ISMS-P `●` |
| Korean source | 1.2.1 정보자산 식별 |

## Certification criterion

In accordance with the organization's business characteristics, information asset classification criteria shall be established to identify/classify all information assets within the scope of the management system, determine their importance, and then keep the asset inventory up to date.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are information asset classification criteria established, and are all assets within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system identified and managed as an inventory?
2. For the identified information assets, is importance determined and a security grade assigned by considering legal requirements and the impact on business operations, among other factors?
3. Is the status of information assets surveyed periodically to keep the information asset inventory up to date?

## Detailed explanation

**Information asset classification criteria shall be established, and all assets within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system shall be identified and managed as an inventory.**

- Establish information asset classification criteria suited to the organization's characteristics, and identify all information assets without omission according to the classification criteria

  ※ Information asset classification (example)
  - Classification by asset type: servers, data (DBMS), information systems (application programs), software, network equipment, security systems, PCs, information, facilities, physical premises, etc.
  - Items by asset type (examples)
    - Server: host name, asset serial number, model name, purpose, IP address, managing department name, operational manager, responsible manager, security grade, etc.
    - Data: database name, table name, (personal) information item names (e.g., name, gender, date of birth, mobile phone number, email, etc.), managing department name, operational manager, responsible manager, storage system (host name), storage location (IP address), security grade, etc.
    - Information system: hardware and software necessary for the collection, processing, storage, retrieval, and transmission/reception of information, such as servers, terminals (e.g., PCs), auxiliary storage media, network equipment, and application programs
    - Security system: systems built to prevent the damage, alteration, or leakage of information, including intrusion prevention (firewall) systems, intrusion detection systems, intrusion prevention systems, and personal information leakage prevention systems
    - Information: includes both documentary information and electronic information (important information, personal information, etc.)

- Verify asset information such as asset name, purpose, location, responsible manager and operator, and managing department, and prepare an inventory

- For efficient management of information assets, use an asset management system or manage the assets in various forms such as documents (spreadsheets)

- When using cloud services, prepare classification criteria that reflect the characteristics of cloud services (for example, virtual servers, object storage, etc.), and identify/manage cloud assets accordingly

**For the identified information assets, importance shall be determined and a security grade assigned by considering legal requirements and the impact on business operations, among other factors.**

- Determine security grade evaluation criteria suited to each asset's characteristics, such as legal requirements and the impact on business operations

  ※ Security grade assessment criteria (example)
  - Evaluate importance according to confidentiality, integrity, availability, legal compliance, etc.
  - Also consider service impact, loss of profit, loss of customers, damage to external image, etc.

- Assess the security grade for each information asset according to the security grade evaluation criteria and manage it as an inventory

**The status of information assets shall be surveyed periodically to keep the information asset inventory up to date.**

- Establish procedures so that the status of newly introduced, changed, and disposed assets can be verified

- Perform periodic surveys of the information asset status and keep the information asset inventory up to date

## Related laws

_No individual legal provision directly corresponding to this item is specified._

## Evidence (examples)

- Information asset and personal information asset classification criteria
- Information asset and personal information asset inventory (asset management system screen)
- Information asset and personal information security grades
- Asset inspection records
- Risk analysis report (asset identification records)

## Nonconformity examples

- A case in which internal information leakage control systems, such as print output security, document encryption, and USB media control used to control the PCs of important-information handlers and personal-information handlers, are omitted from the asset inventory within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system
- A case in which there is personal information provided by a third party within the scope of the information security and personal information protection management system, but asset identification for that personal information has not been carried out
- A case in which the information asset and personal information security grade classification criteria specified in internal guidelines do not match the classification criteria in the asset management ledger
- A case in which on-premise assets have been identified, but asset identification for externally outsourced IT services (web hosting, server hosting, cloud, etc.) is omitted (where such services are within the certification scope)
- A case in which the rationality and reliability of information asset importance assessment are insufficient, such as assigning a confidentiality grade of "low" to a backup server that stores personal information such as unique identifying information
