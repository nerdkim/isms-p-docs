# 3.5.1 Disclosure of the Privacy Policy

## Certification criterion

A privacy policy that includes all necessary matters, such as the purposes of processing personal data, must be established so that data subjects can readily understand it, and it must be disclosed by appropriate means so that data subjects can easily check it at any time and be kept continuously up to date.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is the privacy policy written specifically and clearly in easy-to-understand terms, including all matters required by law?
2. Is the privacy policy disclosed on the internet website, etc., in a manner kept continuously up to date so that data subjects can easily check it?
3. When the privacy policy is changed, are the reasons and the details of the change announced without delay, and are measures taken so that data subjects can easily identify the changes at any time?

## Detailed explanation

**The privacy policy must be written specifically and clearly in easy-to-understand terms, including all matters required by law.**

- Mandatory matters to be included in the privacy policy (refer to Article 30 of the Personal Information Protection Act and Article 31 of its Enforcement Decree)
  1. The purposes of processing personal data
  2. The processing and retention periods of personal data
  3. Matters concerning the provision of personal data to third parties (specified only where applicable.)
  4. The procedures and methods for destroying personal data (where personal data must be retained pursuant to the proviso of Article 21(1) of the Act, this includes the grounds for retention and the personal data items retained.)
  5. The possibility of disclosure of sensitive information under Article 23(3) of the Act and the method of choosing non-disclosure (specified only where applicable.)
  6. Matters concerning the outsourcing of personal data processing (specified only where applicable.)
  7. Matters concerning the processing of pseudonymized information under Articles 28-2 and 28-3 of the Act (specified only where applicable.)
  8. Matters concerning the rights/obligations of data subjects and legal representatives and how to exercise them
  9. The name of the personal data protection officer under Article 31, or the name, telephone number, and other contact details of the department that handles personal data protection tasks and related grievances
  10. Matters concerning the installation/operation of devices that automatically collect personal data, such as internet access information files, and how to refuse them (specified only where applicable.)
  11. The items of personal data processed
  12. Matters concerning measures to ensure the safety of personal data under Article 30 of the Enforcement Decree

- Other matters to be included in the privacy policy (Article 19 of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines)
  1. Where cross-border transfer is necessary in order to outsource the processing/storage of personal data pursuant to Article 28-8(1)3 of the Act, the matters set out in each subparagraph of Article 28-8(2) of the Act (specified only where applicable)
  2. Matters concerning changes to the privacy policy
  3. Where a domestic representative is designated pursuant to Article 31-2(1) of the Act, the name, address, telephone number, and email address of the domestic representative (specified only where applicable)
  4. Matters concerning the rights/obligations of data subjects and legal representatives and how to exercise them, such as the right to request access to, correction/deletion of, and suspension of processing of personal data
  5. The department that receives/handles requests for access to personal data
  6. Remedies for infringement of data subjects' rights and interests

- For personal data that may be processed without the consent of the data subject, disclose in the privacy policy the items and the legal basis for processing them, distinguishing them from personal data processed with the consent of the data subject

- The contents of the privacy policy, such as the purposes of processing personal data, the items of personal data processed, and matters concerning provision to third parties, must match the actual status of personal data processing, and must be written specifically and clearly in easy-to-understand terms reflecting the characteristics of the service and the data subjects
  1. Establish specifically and appropriately the matters that the Act requires to be included in the privacy policy, such as the grounds for processing personal data and the guarantee of data subjects' rights
  2. Establish the privacy policy in clear and easy-to-understand language so that data subjects can readily understand it

- Where an exception under the Personal Information Protection Act applies, the privacy policy need not be established

  * Personal data files exempt from the requirement to establish a privacy policy (public institutions)
  1. Personal data files recording matters concerning national security, diplomatic secrets, or other significant national interests
  2. Personal data files recording matters concerning criminal investigation, the institution and maintenance of public prosecution, the execution of punishment and custody, correctional dispositions, protective dispositions, security surveillance dispositions, and immigration control
  3. Personal data files recording matters concerning the investigation of offenses under the "Punishment of Tax Evaders Act" and the investigation of offenses under the "Customs Act"
  4. Personal data files that are recognized as having a low need for continuous management, such as files operated on a one-off basis, as prescribed by Presidential Decree
     - Personal data files operated for the performance of simple tasks, such as paying attendance allowances for meetings, sending materials/goods, or settling money, that have a low need for continuous management
     - Personal data files processed temporarily where urgently necessary for public safety and welfare, such as public health
     - Other personal data files collected solely for the one-off handling of tasks that are not stored or recorded
  5. Personal data files classified as confidential under other statutes

**Accessibility must be ensured so that data subjects can easily check the privacy policy, and it must be disclosed on the internet website, etc., in a manner kept continuously up to date.**

- Use the standardized designation "privacy policy"

- Where it is disclosed on the front page of the internet website, display it using font size, color, etc., to distinguish it from other notices so that data subjects can easily check it

- Where an internet website is not operated, the privacy policy may be disclosed through other methods prescribed by law

  * Methods of disclosing the privacy policy where it cannot be posted on an internet website (Article 31(3) of the Enforcement Decree)
  - Posting it in a conspicuous place at the business site, etc., of the personal data controller
  - Publishing it in the official gazette (only where the personal data controller is a public institution) or in a general daily newspaper, general weekly newspaper, or internet newspaper under Article 2(1)(a)/(c) and Article 2(2) of the "Act on the Promotion of Newspapers, etc." whose main distribution area is a region at or above the level of the city/province where the personal data controller's business site, etc., is located
  - Continuously publishing it in a publication/newsletter/promotional material issued at least twice a year under the same title and distributed to data subjects, or in bills, etc.
  - Publishing it in a contract, etc., prepared by the personal data controller and the data subject for the provision of goods or services and issuing it to the data subject

**When the privacy policy is changed, the reasons and the details of the change must be announced without delay, and measures must be taken so that data subjects can easily identify the changes at any time.**

- Disclose the changes by comparing the before and after so that data subjects can easily check the changes at any time

  * Methods of announcing the reasons for and details of changes to the privacy policy (examples)
  - Announcing them through the notices section on the front page of the internet website or through a separate window
  - Announcing them to data subjects in writing/by fax/by email or by a similar method
  - Writing them on a notice or placing them in a conspicuous place within the store/office

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 30 (Establishment and Disclosure of the Privacy Policy), Article 30-2 (Evaluation of and Recommendations for Improvement of the Privacy Policy)

## Evidence (examples)

- Privacy policy
- Records of announcements regarding amendments to the privacy policy (bulletin board, etc.)

## Nonconformity examples

- Where the personal data collection and third-party provision details disclosed in the privacy policy differ from the details actually collected and provided
- Where changes have occurred in the contents disclosed in the privacy policy, such as a change of the personal data protection officer or a change of the outsourced processor, but the policy has not been updated to reflect them
- Where the privacy policy is disclosed, but its designation is not "privacy policy" but "personal data protection policy," and it is not displayed using font size, color, etc., so that data subjects can easily find it
- Where the privacy policy has been amended several times, but the contents of previously prepared privacy policies are not disclosed in a way that allows them to be checked
- Where personal data is retained for a certain period without being destroyed pursuant to other statutes such as the E-Commerce Act or the Commercial Act, but the grounds for such retention and the personal data items retained are not disclosed in the privacy policy
