# 3.4.1 Destruction of Personal Information

| Field | Value |

## Certification criterion

Internal policies related to the retention period and destruction of personal information shall be established, and when the destruction point is reached, such as when the retention period of personal information has elapsed or the processing purpose has been achieved, the personal information shall be destroyed without delay in a manner that ensures the safety and completeness of destruction.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are internal policies related to the retention period and destruction of personal information established?
2. When the processing purpose of personal information has been achieved or the retention period has elapsed, is the relevant personal information destroyed without delay?
3. When destroying personal information, is it destroyed in a safe manner so that it cannot be recovered or reproduced?
4. Are records of the destruction of personal information kept and managed?

## Detailed explanation

Internal policies related to the retention period and destruction of personal information shall be established including the following matters.

- By collected item, by collection purpose, and by collection channel: storage location (databases, backup data, etc.), destruction method, destruction point, legal basis, etc.
- Public institutions shall establish and implement a personal information destruction plan reflecting the retention period, processing purpose, etc. of the personal information file, and the personal information destruction plan may be included in the internal management plan (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines Article 55(2))

When the processing purpose of personal information has been achieved or the retention period has elapsed, the relevant personal information shall be destroyed without delay.

- When the personal information becomes unnecessary, such as achievement of the processing purpose, abolition of the relevant service, termination of the business, or elapse of the retention period under laws and regulations, the personal information shall be destroyed within 5 days from that time unless there is a justifiable reason

  ※ Cases where the purpose of collecting and using personal information has been achieved (examples)
  / When the data subject withdraws membership from a website
  / When the data subject cancels a high-speed internet service
  / When the data subject requests withdrawal from mileage membership
  / When an event collecting personal information has ended
  / When the telemarketing (TM) work of a third-party company has ended after providing information to that company for telemarketing purposes, etc.

When destroying personal information, it shall be destroyed in a safe manner so that it cannot be recovered or reproduced. Here, a method by which restoration is impossible means a method of taking measures so that the restoration of the destroyed personal information is impossible at a socially acceptable reasonable cost given the current level of technology.

- Complete destruction (incineration/shredding, etc.)
- Deletion using dedicated demagnetizing equipment (equipment that deletes data on a storage device using a magnetic field)
- Performing initialization or overwriting so that data cannot be restored

  ※ Destruction methods (examples)
  / Complete destruction : Destroying paper documents such as membership application forms containing personal information, or hard disks or magnetic tapes, by shredding, dissolving, or burning them in an incinerator or incineration facility, etc.
  / When using dedicated demagnetizing equipment : Deleting personal information stored on hard disks or magnetic tapes using a degausser, etc.
  / When performing initialization or overwriting so that data cannot be restored : Using methods such as a full format (3 or more times recommended) of the hard disk storing personal information, overwriting the data area with random values, 0s, 1s, etc. (3 or more times recommended), or storing the relevant drive encrypted with a secure algorithm and key length and then deleting it, followed by complete disposal of the key used for encryption and overwriting with random values, etc.

- When destroying only part of the personal information and it is difficult to destroy by the above methods, the following measures shall be implemented
  1. In the case of an electronic file form : After deleting the personal information, manage and supervise it so that it cannot be recovered or reproduced
  2. In the case of records, printed materials, documents, or other recording media other than an electronic file form : Delete the relevant part by masking, punching holes, etc.
- When it is significantly difficult to destroy by the above methods due to technical characteristics, take measures so that restoration is impossible by processing the information as information falling under Article 58-2 of the Act (anonymized processing)

Records of the destruction of personal information shall be kept and managed.

- The implementation of the destruction of personal information and the confirmation of the destruction results shall be carried out under the responsibility of the personal information protection officer, and matters concerning destruction shall be recorded/managed
- Records of the destruction of personal information shall be recorded in a destruction management ledger, or photographs, etc. documenting the destruction shall be kept as records
- When a public institution destroys a personal information file, it shall confirm the destruction results and prepare a personal information file destruction management ledger (Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines Article 55)

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 21 (Destruction of Personal Information)
- Standards for Measures to Ensure the Safety of Personal Information Article 13 (Destruction of Personal Information)

## Evidence (examples)

- Regulations related to the retention period and destruction of personal information
- Results of personal information destruction (member database, etc.)
- Personal information destruction management ledger

## Nonconformity examples

- A case where, when the purpose has been achieved or the retention period has elapsed such as membership withdrawal, the relevant personal information was destroyed from the member database, but the personal information replicated and stored in linked personal information processing systems such as CRM/DW was not destroyed
- A case where, for personal information collected while running an event during a specific period, destruction criteria have not been established or destruction is not carried out even after the event has ended
- A case where personal information related to complaint handling collected at a call center (consultation history, recordings, etc.) is retained for 3 years based on the Electronic Commerce Act, but is retained without being destroyed even after 3 years have elapsed
- A case where, because complete destruction of personal information whose purpose has been achieved is difficult due to technical characteristics such as blockchain, anonymized processing was performed instead of complete destruction, but the anonymized processing was not performed appropriately, so re-identification and other restoration of some personal information is possible
