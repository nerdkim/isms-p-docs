# 3.2.1 Personal Information Status Management

| Section | 3.2 Protective Measures When Holding and Using Personal Information |

## Certification criterion

The status of collected/held personal information, including the items, quantity held, purpose and method of processing, and retention period, shall be managed on a regular basis, and in the case of public institutions, this shall be registered with the head of the relevant agency designated by law.

## Key checkpoints

1. Is the status of collected/held personal information, including the items, quantity held, purpose and method of processing, and retention period, managed on a regular basis?
2. When a public institution operates or changes a personal information file, are the related matters registered with the head of the relevant agency designated by law?
3. Does the public institution disclose the retention status of personal information files in its privacy policy?

## Detailed explanation

The status of collected/held personal information, including the items, quantity held, purpose and method of processing, and retention period, shall be managed on a regular basis.

- The personal information controller (information and communications service provider) shall identify the items of collected/held personal information, the quantity held, the basis for processing (consent, statutes, etc.), the purpose and method of processing, and the retention period, and shall record/manage them through a personal information status table, a personal information flow table, a personal information flow diagram, etc.
- In addition, the personal information status shall be reviewed on a regular basis and the related documents shall be kept up to date.

When a public institution operates or changes a personal information file, the related matters shall be registered with the head of the relevant agency designated by law, and where there is a change to the registered matters, that content shall also be registered.

- Upon receiving an application to register or change a personal information file, the Chief Privacy Officer reviews the registration/change matters and determines their appropriateness, then registers them with the Personal Information Protection Commission within 60 days.
- Institutions affiliated with central administrative agencies and local governments, and other public institutions, request the higher-level supervising agency to review and determine the appropriateness of the relevant matters (registration/change of personal information files), and after receiving confirmation from the higher-level supervising agency, register them with the Personal Information Protection Commission within 60 days.
- The registration and disclosure of personal information files of the National Assembly, the courts, the Constitutional Court, and the National Election Commission (including their affiliated agencies) shall follow the National Assembly Rules, the Supreme Court Rules, the Constitutional Court Rules, and the National Election Commission Rules.
- However, personal information files falling under Article 32(2) of the Personal Information Protection Act need not be registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission.

**Personal information files exempt from registration with the Personal Information Protection Commission (public institutions)**

1. Personal information files recording matters concerning national security, diplomatic secrets, or other significant national interests
2. Personal information files recording matters concerning criminal investigation, the institution and maintenance of prosecution, the execution of sentences and custody, correctional dispositions, protective dispositions, protective custody dispositions, and immigration control
3. Personal information files recording matters concerning the investigation of offenses under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the investigation of offenses under the Customs Act
4. Personal information files prescribed by Presidential Decree as being of low necessity for continuous management, such as files operated on a one-time basis
   - Personal information files operated for the performance of simple tasks such as payment of allowances for meeting attendance, sending of materials/goods, and settlement of money, for which the necessity of continuous management is low
   - Personal information files processed temporarily in cases urgently required for public safety and welfare, such as public health
   - Other personal information files collected only for one-time task processing that are not stored or recorded
5. Personal information files classified as secret under other statutes
6. Personal information files collected or requested to be provided for the purpose of analyzing information related to national security
7. Personal image information files processed through image information processing devices
8. Personal information files held by financial institutions under the Act on Real Name Financial Transactions and Confidentiality for the handling of financial business

Public institutions shall disclose the retention status of personal information files in their privacy policy.

- The Chief Privacy Officer of a public institution periodically investigates the retention/destruction status of personal information files and discloses the results in the privacy policy of the relevant public institution (Article 61 of the Standard Personal Information Protection Guidelines).
- The Personal Information Protection Commission discloses the registration status of personal information files on the internet so that anyone can easily view it (Personal Information Portal, www.privacy.go.kr).

## Related laws

- Personal Information Protection Act Article 32 (Registration and Disclosure of Personal Information Files)

## Evidence (examples)

- Personal information status table
- Personal information flow table/flow diagram
- Personal information file registration status
- Personal information file management ledger
- Privacy policy

## Nonconformity examples

- The case where personal information files are managed as a list through the personal information file registration menu on the website, but the content of some personal information files related to website services is omitted from the privacy policy
- The case where two months have passed since a new personal information file was established, but the personal information file has not been registered with the Personal Information Protection Commission
- The case where the content of a personal information file registered and disclosed with the Personal Information Protection Commission (such as the items of personal information collected) differs from the actual status of the personal information file being processed
- The case where a public institution has not registered personal information files of its executives and employees, or personal information files collected under the Statistics Act, with the Personal Information Protection Commission, even though they do not fall under the exceptions to personal information file registration
